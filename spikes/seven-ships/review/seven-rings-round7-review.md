## Changes that visibly landed

- Capital hero framing now includes the whole ship. Carrier no longer loses its bow at the right border. There is now somewhat more margin than the targets, particularly above/left, but fixing the clipping was necessary.
- Capital rings have more substantial plated volume than round 6; their silhouettes are more convincing. Pilgrim radiators and tiny windows are now genuinely visible, breaking up some formerly empty shell.
- Hard edges separate more crisply and metallic pieces have clearer highlights. This is useful but does not substitute for target-scale construction.
- Bastion bay detail now actually shows the interior. The back wall is articulated and lit rather than blank; two side catwalks, railings, ladders, floor cabinets and overhead machinery are visible.

## Explorer / Pilgrim — 5.10

Composition remains essentially unchanged. The target's axial engine assembly projects strongly left beyond the ring; here it is compressed in projection and occluded by the foreground rim. Target ring cross-section remains visually deeper and richer, and its spokes have dense trussed mechanical construction instead of sparse straps across paired bars. Expose more aft assembly through camera/assembly consideration, respecting agreed physical dimensions.

Visible ring vents/windows are progress, but are still small repeated decorations along a largely uniform tile grid. The target has large black radiator fields, broad layered bands, irregular service bays and inhabited strips integrated into the ring shell. Increase those medium-scale zones, add depth/rebates, and avoid applying every detail on the same repeated pitch. The target hub is a segmented mechanical assembly; current hub is mostly a smooth dark cylinder with a gold disk.

Lighting is crisper but target warmth and inhabited luminosity remain missing. Lower-left and far-side ring are darker; tiny isolated light slits do not create the target's warm window/service-light pattern. Bring back warm localized cues and enough neutral fill to separate shadow hardware, without flattening the key light. Cockpit glazing remains too opaque and uniformly teal. Target has much denser stars, though hull work is the priority.

## Carrier / Bastion — 5.20

Ring is improved, but its broad exposed inner surface still reads as two smooth dark hoops and empty space. The target combines substantial segmented inner habitat walls, repeated light rows, large radiator faces and visible articulated support roots. Fill that visual vocabulary, not the clearance volume: preserve verified rotation clearance and floor diameter. Ring circumference also retains broad uniform color stripes where target paint is interrupted by differently sized machinery/armor zones.

Target side armor is broken into nested housings, inclined vent caps, dark recesses, service windows and varied equipment. Current side repeats one tall tiled slab, two small grille patches, horizontal tubing and square cabinets across nearly the entire length. Introduce a few genuinely different bay assemblies with shared construction language. The large forward collar still has expanses of tiled cream plate with almost no target-like fixtures. Central bridge remains a stepped pyramid with little glazing; make the existing bridge low, glazed and mechanically specific. Do not restore the removed deck fairings.

Lighting moved farther from target in the bay and lower sides. Cool roof highlights help separation, but the approved target has warm, readable interior volume; hero now shows a dark box. Add warm wall/task light with actual nearby surface illumination and adequate fill; emissive rectangles alone are insufficient. The roof still reads like sparse parking spaces and cubes on a flat sheet; target has richer deck edge hardware, inset surface zones and operational markings.

### Bastion interior: user's articulation request

This is a substantive improvement in inspectability and wall coverage. The new detail proves that the back wall has equipment rather than relying on inference from the exterior.

However, the dominant rear-wall treatment is a near-uniform grid of large framed black horizontal grilles. It resembles repeated ventilation panels rather than a functioning shipyard. The large side cabinets also repeat an identical three-slat front. Scale diversity and functional relationships are still missing.

Replace selected grille cells with a recessed access door, vertical service manifolds, control cabinets, cable trays and fitted storage units; run a few visible service lines between them. Keep some grilles as ventilation, but avoid turning every cell into the same prop. Model the crane/trolley structure with a readable rail and truss hierarchy instead of principally plain rods/boxes. Give railing posts proper feet, cabinets fitted mounts, and floor equipment a plausible arrangement beside the clear central route. Let warm task lighting reveal the under-catwalk structure.

Current detail camera is a tight, symmetrical frontal crop: it cuts the outer catwalks and much of the crane and gives little side-wall depth. Keep this as rear-wall evidence, and add a wider front-oblique interior shot that includes the mouth frame, one full side catwalk, crane span, floor and rear wall. That is the strongest way to demonstrate the shipyard-inspired interior to the user.

## Dreadnought — 4.25

Framing and ring volume improve, but the nose and weapon assemblies still dominate the discrepancy. The target has interlocking tapered armor planes, deep nose service channels, a layered illuminated muzzle, compact turrets with rounded/beveled housings and fitted recoil machinery. Current nose remains a large tiled blank shell ending in a dark circular hole surrounded by plain blocks. Current turret bodies remain simple rectangular slabs on long exposed barrels and mounts.

This requires changing assemblies, not adding more uniform scratches or small bevels. Split the nose into substantial armor masses around recessed channels; build the muzzle as nested mechanical rings with appropriate warm interior light; add service recesses and broken pale/red armor zones. Rebuild turret housings with stepped/beveled side plates, barrel collars and recessed mounting pockets. Differentiate military side hardware from the carrier's repeated cabinet-and-pipe facade.

New lighting gives cooler top highlights but loses more side and ring information to near-black. Target is dark but contains abundant pale armor, warm window strips and readable layered mechanical depth. Restore that value/color hierarchy through differentiated materials plus restrained warm local light and fill. Global exposure alone will not solve the missing zones.

## Stall assessment and next decision

**Stall approaching is triggered:** the same main gaps recur in two consecutive reviews—uniform medium-scale armor, simplistic capital bridge/turret/nose assemblies, insufficient habitat-ring machinery, and under-lit sparse bay construction. The gains of +0.20/+0.35/+0.05 do not justify another round limited to bevels, detail visibility, torus thickness or light tuning.

The work did improve, so this review does not label it fully stalled under the rule requiring an unsuccessful major architectural attempt with equal/worse score. The next attempt should be a deliberate reconstruction of selected high-impact assemblies: dreadnought nose/turrets, capital ring inhabited shell and Bastion's varied internal service architecture. If that is not in scope, show the present verified result to the principal and state honestly that target fidelity remains well below 8 rather than presenting a pass.
