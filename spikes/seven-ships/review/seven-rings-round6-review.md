## Explorer / Pilgrim

- Composition: overall ring/spine concept is recognizable. Camera makes the axial body much shorter in projection and hides the aft pods behind the ring; target shows engines projecting conspicuously left of the ring. Rotate view or adjust framing to expose the aft assembly. Ring is slimmer and less substantial in cross-section, with fewer visibly distinct construction layers; keep the 80 m floor diameter but increase visible shell articulation/depth toward target. Target spokes are framed open trusses with substantial bolted ends; current spokes are paired pipes with periodic simple straps. Hub currently reads as a smooth black cylinder and mustard washer instead of segmented service/rotation machinery.
- Lighting: target has warm pin lights, broad cream highlights, readable shadow detail, and local metallic edge glints. Current warm lighting is confined to sparse spoke slits; the ring has almost no lit inhabited cues. Add window/service-light rows and restrained emissive glow, and tune rim/fill so machinery separates from space. Background has markedly fewer stars than target.
- Materials: large uninterrupted cream/mustard ring zones with a fine, regular tile grid dominate the image. Target alternates inset black radiator rectangles, raised bands, irregular service-panel blocks, blue windows, interrupted painted panels and fastening strips. Those broad features must exist before micro-noise matters. Make ochre patches chipped and panel-bounded rather than pristine circumferential stripes. Cockpit glass is a solid opaque teal wedge; add frame depth, varied pane response and reflections consistent with target.
- Details: ring vents/windows are the first priority, then hub clamps, doubled service lines, engine cap depth, spoke mechanical junctions and smaller hull access fixtures. Present cockpit and dish are identifiable, but simplified.

## Carrier / Bastion

- Composition: hero crops the forward/right end against the image edge, while target fits the full ship comfortably. Pull back/recenter before the next judged capture. Target ring is a massive inhabited structure with broad plated sidewalls; current ring is a skinny perimeter hoop with two especially prominent smooth black inner hoses. Increase layered cross-section and replace hose-dominant appearance with segmented inner wall, bearings and planted spoke roots, retaining 180 m floor diameter. Main body concept and forward hangar remain recognizable. Bridge is a stepped, largely blank white ziggurat; target bridge is a low, glazed service structure. This is about the surviving central bridge, not the intentionally removed deck fairings.
- Lighting: target hangar is luminous, warm and spatially legible; current bay is predominantly dark with a large black rear plane. Illuminate wall-mounted service lights and crane/catwalk surfaces, with plausible restrained spill. Exterior lacks target rows of warm windows and broad shadow/highlight variation. Current roof reads as flat brown-grey road surface.
- Materials: cream armor is too continuous, with all side bays sharing the same repeated pipe/vent/cabinet motif. Target has recessed black machinery strips, patchwork armor, differently sized access panels and chunky bevels breaking each bay. Front collar is especially blank: add inset vents, corner armor, lamps and nested frame rebates. Roof needs differentiated deck panels, service strips, border machinery and finer operational markings rather than a single sparse parking-lot layout. Do not restore deleted cockpit fairings.
- Interior articulation: meaningful progress is visible. Front/detail show two raised side catwalks, handrails, columns, cabinets, a crane beam and suspended hook. These are correctly in the shipyard vocabulary. However, a broad unarticulated rear wall and mostly empty floor dominate the front view. Side cabinets are large rounded black boxes with three flat orange slats; rails/crane are bare thin rods; there are few structural depth layers. Add wall ribs, inset access doors, pipes/cable trays, fitted equipment at more than one scale, a trussed crane with trolley and rails, wall-mounted worklights, and small floor service fixtures around the clear route. Break the repeated symmetric box staging. Preserve the useful unobstructed central passage.
- Capture quality: current detail view mostly inspects the outer hull and front corner. A closer front-oblique bay view would show whether the full internal depth is actually articulated; current front image makes the entire ship only about one-quarter of image height.

## Dreadnought

- Composition: ring silhouette makes the approved new concept recognizable, but shares carrier's excessively thin shell/black-hose construction. Target hull tapers and layers into a complex armored nose; current nose is an enormous blank tiled rounded box ending in a black tube with eight plain cube-like lugs. Rebuild the nose into interlocking armor planes, inset channels and articulated emitter housing. Keep overall scale commitments intact. Target top batteries are compact twin-barrel turrets on recessed mounting machinery; current flat rectangular turret blocks and long widely exposed pipes feel loose and oversized. Blend mounts into deck pockets, bevel casings, and articulate recoil housings. Current bridge repeats the same stepped capital pyramid, reducing target's specific silhouette.
- Lighting: target is dark gunmetal but still strongly separated into charcoal plates, pale armor, warm windows and lit muzzle/service cavities. Current ship is uniformly dim; small ribs are lit, but almost every large area sits at similar dark value. Add target-like cream armor islands, longitudinal warm window bands and a restrained amber muzzle interior; expose bevels with key/rim light. Do not solve with global brightening alone.
- Materials: regular tile grid dominates nose and upper hull. Target has a purposeful mix of large armor polygons, medium seams, radiator faces, edge wear and tiny fixings. Current repetitive side equipment is virtually carrier hardware recolored. Give military hull thicker inset armor, different module widths, integrated turrets and red wear patches. Smooth black ring tubing and round muzzle lip contrast with target's mechanically layered frames.
- Details: missing nose-side service recesses, windows, warning markings, inset turret joints, barrel collars, split top channels and dense ring radiators. Prioritize nose silhouette/material blocking, turret architecture and ring habitation over additional uniform panel lines.
