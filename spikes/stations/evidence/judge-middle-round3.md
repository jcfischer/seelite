## Wheel

The closer framing now fills the image convincingly. A thicker rim, small hub fins, concentric hub face relief and warm mouth illumination improve recognizability. However, the rim now reads as a continuous smooth white band with an almost uninterrupted light strip. The target has broad individual inhabited segments with substantial transverse joints, irregular equipment and dark axial depth. The central hub still reads as a box with thin concentric trims instead of a layered barrel. Spokes retain the stark pale-rail/black-rail split.

Priority changes:

1. Break the continuous rim light strip into shorter groups with dark interruptions. Add deep projecting transverse joints and some larger plate-value changes; the target ring has discrete structural bays, not a luminous belt.
2. Add a visibly wider rear collar to the hub and greater separation between the mouth frame and outer hull. Increase fin width and arrange them so their faces, not just thin edges, register in this view.
3. Brighten spoke conduits locally to a mid-dark metal value and give their casings more depth. The upper and lower vertical machinery columns still lose nearly all structure into black.
4. Vary the rear ring with attached boxes, warm local lamps and occasional plate covers. Its repeated triangular truss currently looks much emptier than the target.
5. Keep the improved framing and mouth warmth. Add seam-localized wear and selective edge highlights rather than more uniform mottling. The newly visible stars help, although their uniformly tiny sharp points remain unlike the target field.

## Derrick

Domed tank shoulders fix a major prior mismatch, and framing is significantly closer. The landing rectangle is legible. But the top pad has changed from dark empty metal to pale mottled concrete-like material; this is not the target landing surface. Tanks remain oversized simple vessels with black disks where the target has raised valve stacks, pipe elbows and platforms. The base is still a plain pale slab. The thin upper fascia and sparse hut remain conspicuous.

Priority changes:

1. Darken only the landing surface to a medium-dark worn charcoal metal while retaining the new inner rectangle. Give the rectangle worn white/yellow breaks and vary a few large plate values. Preserve visibility without making the entire pad pale.
2. Replace black tank-top disks with raised necks, valve heads and supported elbow pipes that visibly enter the service core. Add small railing/platform sections near the shoulders.
3. Deepen the deck fascia, especially its front face; add large inset grilles and vertical corner brackets extending below the current thin edge. The target has considerable machinery volume beneath the landing plane.
4. Add a dark machinery belt, corner guards and rails to the lower plinth. Its uninterrupted white rim currently dominates the base.
5. Give the central core several longer warm fixtures and layered panels behind the braces. Keep dark gaps, but reveal enough midtone structure to avoid the central column reading as an empty cage.
6. Articulate the hut with a door, side equipment and several slim antennae. Its bright four-window cube is too simple and domestically scaled relative to the target industrial cabin.

## Buoy

The station is appropriately larger in frame. Removing the obstructing front tray exposes the enlarged hatch; the structural piers and upper shoulders improve body shape. Asymmetric appendages are a better direction. However, the upper radiator visibly appears to float above the roof: no substantial connection to the deck is visible. Both upper and lower radiators face the viewer with their backs/vertical framing and yellow blocks rather than the target ribbed faces; their orientations remain wrong. The bright hatch strips and pale pad overpower otherwise dark machinery. The lower body still reads as a plain drum with small equipment pasted along it.

Priority changes:

1. Fix the raised radiator attachment first: use visible hinges and struts rooted in the roof, with no black gap suggesting a floating panel. Tilt it backward and expose its broad ribbed face. Give the bottom panel a downward tilt with its ribbed face visible as well; remove the conspicuous yellow blocks from these viewing faces.
2. Darken the pad to worn charcoal metal, preserving the landing markings. The target has a dark landing plane and pale hull; the current two pale areas merge visually.
3. Reduce hatch strip intensity and break each into smaller warm fixtures. Add dark beveled outer brackets, top pipework and stepped depth so the hatch reads as mechanical construction, not a flat luminous picture frame.
4. Replace some tiny repeated lower equipment with a few substantial horizontal tanks and larger inset boxes separated by dark gaps. This should alter the lower silhouette and depth, not merely add texture to the cylinder.
5. Give the side radiator a thick frame, visible ribs and hinge supports. It currently reads as a thin dark shelf.
6. Add diagonal support brackets between deck corners and hull, plus visible pipe elbows beside the piers. The target roof/body junction is an assembled load-bearing structure rather than a slab resting directly on a rounded shoulder.

## Shared assessment

Round 3 makes real progress in framing and primary forms. Remaining work is concentrated in mechanical depth, connections and localized lighting. More global brightness or speckle will not resolve it. Wheel is closest to threshold; Derrick needs its deck material and service hardware corrected; Buoy needs its radiator mounting/orientation fixed before further polishing.
