## Rig

Shorter, broader drum and a more compact brace span move toward the target. The extended stepped drill and articulated jaws now convey the intended mechanism. The gray deck retains useful separation from painted hull. These visible changes justify composition and detail gains.

1. **Repair the diagonal brace surfaces.** Round5 has visible jagged pale shards and thin projecting slivers along/below the large front diagonal member. Its uneven broken surface is conspicuous against black space. Make its flanged rails and filled structural panels coherent, with clean connections at both ends; inspect from under and side views as well.
2. **Make the shoulder one connected transition.** The new sloped grille in front of the crown reads as a separate tilted plate perched at deck level. Target has an integrated sloping mechanical shoulder joining bridge to upper housing. Narrow the tall square crown, lower its visual mass, and connect the shoulder with side cheeks and recessed machinery.
3. **Refine the drill and jaw materials.** The drill is now long enough but its stacked cylinders terminate in a flat-ended rod, rather than a visibly pointed bit. The jaws have articulation but remain broad flat cream slabs. Add dark recessed sides, metallic hinge rims and tapered inward teeth. Do not enlarge them further.
4. **Restore deck structural depth.** The target has a deep continuous front beam containing horizontal pipe banks. The current pad looks thin, with repeated vent cabinets at its corners. Increase the visible beam depth and place utility pipes inside its face. Larger scuffed deck patches and interrupted broad markings would improve scale more than additional small grilles.
5. **Keep material/light work local.** Uniform brown fine mottling still covers hull, machinery and structure. Separate exposed metallic edges from painted panels; darken grime around seams and give amber fixtures subtle spill onto the drill collar and deck underside. Current emissive dots do not create the target recess illumination.

## Cluster

The left pod is now separated and the upper mast has a terminal assembly, but both expose new visual problems. Connector collars are a useful material boundary on the upper right, although they look like widely separated dark rings rather than fitted flanges. The roof silhouette and three-pod arrangement still identify the intended station.

1. **Fix the left connection before adding detail.** Its central volume appears as a broad dark smeared slab, with long horizontal rails projecting into space above and below. This is not the target short cylindrical corridor. Build one coherent enclosed neck with a readable round flange at each end, a short pale middle and tightly fitted ribs. Remove the loose projecting blades. Verify all adjacent parts moved with the pod.
2. **Replace the mast-top block with a fine aerial.** A large pale rectangular slab carrying a cluster of spikes now caps the lattice. Target continues upward with slender rods and crossmembers; there is no heavy concrete-looking cap. Keep the new height but reduce the cap to small dark clamps and attach rods individually to the truss. Shrink the broad bright horizontal mast lamps.
3. **Tighten the right connector collars.** The upper right pod now has oversized, visibly separated dark discs around its neck. Seat collars directly against the pod and neck, and give them narrower metallic rims. The lower right corridor is still a long featureless pale tube; treat it consistently with small utility runs and dark end flanges.
4. **Complete roof and lower mast proportions.** The target has continuous perimeter railings with upright lamps, two unequal mast-base boxes and a side dish. The current roof remains largely empty with corner lamps, and the lower mast remains short. Add these particular silhouettes rather than another repetition of square vents.
5. **Retain the previous material priorities.** Widen/chip the thin clean yellow roof outline; introduce large gray scuffs, shrink the dominant white wall squares, and light nearby metal with warm spill. The frontal machinery remains a repeated grille border instead of offset service hatches and an underslung rounded pod.

## Salvage

Two visible tower feet reduce the floating-cylinder impression. The pad gains a protruding dark rim, interrupted yellow paint and some distinct patches; the left connection now has a clearer lattice outline. Cockpit rooftop equipment is welcome. These are real but limited improvements: the large silhouette and most surfaces remain very close to round4.

1. **Finish the tower supports as machinery.** The two dark rectangular feet are visible, but read as plain blocks. Add the missing left diagonal support, mechanical collars, and a dense base tied into the roof. The single large right lattice still dominates. Segment and darken the tower rim/interior.
2. **Make pad wear irregular rather than striped.** The border has been broken into fairly regular dashes and the new patches are isolated dark rectangles. Target uses irregular pale chips and broad overlapping abrasion. Vary missing-paint lengths, add larger pale scuffs, and retain the new projecting edge. Restore clearly visible warm upper pad fixtures; they are less legible in round5.
3. **Rebuild cockpit glazing and side silhouette.** It still has two enormous uninterrupted dark panels and three evenly spaced grilles. Introduce wraparound framed window divisions, shorter stepped roof shoulders and differing recessed access panels. The broad bright wedge continues to overpower the target compact salvaged cockpit.
4. **Give the left corridor depth.** New thin bright crossbars outline the connection, but a large plain pod side still fills much of its interior. Use an open, deep lattice corridor with bracing on both sides and visible dark voids; tie the rails into substantial end frames.
5. **Change the lower assembly at the volume level.** It is still a straight stack of repeated cabinet faces with parallel vertical tubes. Offset major volumes, bend plumbing with elbows, add underslung tanks and angle the terminal keel inward. Additional small surface pieces cannot supply the target bulky asymmetric silhouette.
6. **Separate exposed metal and paint.** Lower pipes, cockpit panels and support blocks still share much of the same dull surface response. The existing silver horizontal pipes show the useful direction. Add narrow metallic highlights and warm light falling inside the connections, while reducing large bright ivory side faces.

## Next-round order

- Cluster: rebuild the visibly malformed left neck and remove the heavy mast cap; tighten right flanges.
- Salvage: articulate cockpit glazing and lower volumes; replace regular pad dashes with irregular wear.

Structural cleanup should precede further repeated detail. No score was raised merely to meet the threshold.
