**Cluster narrowly reaches 7; Rig and Salvage do not.** Cluster earns the increase by removing two conspicuous structural defects and adding visible warm light on adjacent surfaces. It remains far from a close match. Rig makes useful deck and brace improvements. Salvage gains light but its new window bars introduce a visible detail problem. Scores are not rounded up to pass.

## Rig

The front deck edge is deeper and more articulated. The formerly jagged lower brace appears substantially cleaner. Paired sloping grille faces add a clearer mechanical transition. Warm spill is visible on the brace and drill collar. The main silhouette, repeated hull grilles and cream jaw blocks remain close to round5; surface treatment has not materially changed.

1. **Finish the brace connections.** A long dark diagonal projects left beyond its junction below the deck. Trim members to substantial end sockets and make the load path visually continuous. The large lower rail still looks like a deep open trough; the target has a plated structural face with narrower exposed ribs.
2. **Integrate the shoulder into the crown.** The paired grille assembly remains a projecting shelf in front of a broad pale collar, while the target shoulder joins bridge and upper housing. Give it connecting side cheeks and reduce the large plain crown side. Avoid adding a third similar grille.
3. **Refine the jaws and bit.** The jaws still read as flat cream zigzags with huge plain circular joints; the bit ends in a blunt rod. Darken recessed jaw sides, add metallic pivot rims and inward tapered teeth, then taper the drill tip. These are prominent silhouette/material mismatches.
4. **Replace deck-edge vent repetition with piping.** The deeper edge is progress, but the target's front has continuous horizontal pipe banks. Recess two or three dark pipes within the beam and reduce the corner grille cabinets' visual dominance.
5. **Vary material at a larger scale.** Fine brown mottling covers nearly every surface. Add broad gray worn patches to the deck, chipped metallic edges on machinery and darker dirt concentrated at seams. More little emissive dots will not resolve this gap.

## Cluster

The left connection is now a coherent enclosed pale neck, with no long loose blades or smeared slab. Removing the bright mast cap restores a slender aerial silhouette. New amber spill on the front vent and lower connector adds genuine local illumination. These specific visible repairs justify recovery beyond round5. The roof, pods and wall equipment otherwise retain their prior limitations.

1. **Seat the collars against the connections.** The upper-right neck still carries large separated black discs; a tall partial disc also rises awkwardly over the left neck near the body. Replace these with fitted narrow flanges at actual joins, each with a thin metal rim.
2. **Restore roof scale cues.** Add continuous perimeter rails and small upright lamps, broaden and chip the yellow outline, and make the two mast-base housings unequal. The target roof is an inhabited service deck; the current broad empty square still dominates.
3. **Reduce the white wall lights.** Large bright white squares flatten the wall hierarchy. Smaller warm fixtures with limited nearby spill would better support the target's quieter detailed panels. Narrow the mast's bright horizontal lamps as well.
4. **Articulate the front and underside.** The front remains a regular frame of repeated grilles around one large dark rectangle. Use offset service hatches, a rounded underslung module and longer lower mast. Add the side aerial/dish at the upper mast base.
5. **Separate connector and pod materials.** The long pale tubes are too clean in form even though their texture is dirty. Fit small utility runs and collar fasteners; vary metallic seams and larger painted panels rather than increasing uniform fine dirt.

## Salvage

Amber spill at the tower base and front frame now visibly lights adjacent metal, an improvement over isolated glowing fixtures. The new side-window dividers, however, resemble external rods dangling below the glass onto the hull rather than fitted mullions. The pad wear, large cockpit wedge and straight lower assembly remain essentially unchanged.

1. **Fix the window bars first.** Shorten and seat each divider within the glazing perimeter, aligned to the window plane. Add actual framed divisions to the upper glass as well. The present projecting rods visibly reduce finish quality.
2. **Reduce and step the cockpit mass.** Its broad bright ivory side still dominates; the target cockpit is more compact, with several stepped roof shoulders and varied recessed panels. Subdivide the volume before adding small surface parts, and widen the subdued rust-red side patch.
3. **Replace pad dashes and dark rectangles with irregular abrasion.** Use overlapping broad pale scuffs and varied missing-paint areas. Restore upper pad-edge fixtures at the pad itself; the new warm fixtures illuminate the surrounding frame instead.
4. **Rebuild the lower silhouette.** It remains a straight column of repeated cabinets and vertical tubes. Offset two major modules, add bent pipes and an underslung tank, and angle the terminal keel inward. Surface repetition cannot substitute for the target's bulky asymmetry.
5. **Complete tower and corridor structure.** Tower feet remain plain blocks and the bright right lattice dominates. Add a left diagonal support and fitted base machinery. The left corridor still shows thin bright braces in front of a large plain pod wall: create depth and visible voids between substantial end frames.
6. **Keep lighting gains controlled.** Warm pools help, but large ivory hull fields and generic grime still dominate. Add darker seams, restrained metal edge response and shadowed recesses rather than increasing overall exposure.

## Priority order

Rig: resolve brace endpoints, integrate shoulder, taper drill and refine jaw materials. Cluster: fit collars, improve roof rails and markings, reduce white fixtures. Salvage: seat window dividers, reshape cockpit and lower volumes, create irregular pad wear. All three would benefit more from distinct structural and material hierarchy than additional repeated vent cabinets.
