## Interceptor

1. **Fill the frame and restore component mass.** Target fills nearly the full image width; current render occupies roughly 60%. Camera direction is recognizable, but rear pods and cockpit are much less substantial relative to the long center spine. Enlarge those masses relative to the spine, and frame the complete ship with target-like margins.
2. **Replace the bare black spine with an armored machinery spine.** Target center is a thick, densely occupied series of off-white armored cylinders, dark collars, copper conduits, side equipment boxes, and recessed machinery. Current central span is mostly a smooth exposed black tube, thin hoops and repeated white blocks. Break the repetition with two or three distinct armored sections, substantial collars, layered exposed piping, and equipment attached to their sides.
3. **Give the four pods depth and correct their ends.** Current pods look like rectangular HVAC units with repeated full-side vents and flat grilles spanning their front faces. Target pods have rounded/chamfered shoulders, broad uninterrupted armor, locally inset side equipment and a narrower dark endcap protruding from a stepped frame. Rear engine bells need more visible layered rim depth.
4. **Rebuild cockpit volume and glazing.** Target has a broad, tall, rounded/chamfered command module, dark blue armor accents, a substantial framed multi-pane canopy extending down the forward slope, and prominent lateral mechanical equipment. Current nose is a small plain wedge with a shallow flat two-pane windshield. Add volume, chamfer stages, inset glass and side assemblies before small surface details.
5. **Appearance.** Replace large mottled black patches and dominant scratchy seam lines with smaller directional chips, fine joints, restrained grime around recesses and varied roughness. Use darker graphite endcaps, stronger metallic collar highlights, cooler blue glass and localized amber fixtures. Current warm pools across blank armor do not substitute for the target lamp housings.

## Patrol

1. **Correct the fuselage silhouette before details.** Target is a thick, broad armored body tapering into a substantial rounded/chamfered nose. Current body becomes a long thin triangular beak and its middle is a flat slab. Shorten that beak, broaden the forward body, deepen its sides, and create several shoulder/bevel transitions into the nose.
2. **Build a stepped dorsal body.** Target has raised central armor terraces, recessed equipment, side shoulders and a prominent framed canopy; current top is largely one uninterrupted plane. Lift and articulate the center deck and cockpit housing, with a wider, longer canopy and visible black framing.
3. **Restore pod and side-channel relationships.** Target nacelles are larger, thicker and more rounded, with pronounced stepped forward caps and exposed pipes/mechanics bridging the gap to the central hull. Current simple vent boxes meet a nearly blank side wall. Put substantial dark channels and differently sized conduits into the gap, plus mounted boxes and collars. Preserve negative space so it reads as layered machinery.
4. **Turrets and dome need manufactured shapes.** Target turret housings are beveled and armored; current guns are small plain blocks. Target dome has metal segments and a dark glazed equatorial band; current is a mottled hemisphere edged by an uninterrupted bright cyan ring. Add segmentation, band thickness, discrete lights and a visible mounting collar.
5. **Appearance and framing.** Increase ship occupancy from about two thirds to nearly the full width. Add the missing broad blue stripe down the upper forward fuselage and restore richer blue pod bands. Use smaller weathering marks, real dark inset vent depths, distinct graphite materials and localized amber fixtures. Current surfaces are too flat, uniform and blotchy despite their texture density.

## Explorer

The large-ring revision is the comparison target. Keep the intended 80 m floor diameter, 84 m overall diameter and 74 m length; the fix is not to shrink the design back to an earlier ring.

1. **Fit the complete ring in the image.** Current ring is cut off at the top and nearly touches the bottom; target has a complete ring with visible margins. Widen the camera framing and adjust its angle so the center spine is less steeply diagonal. Target shows more substantial command and aft modules extending across the ring plane; current ring dominates while those volumes appear small. Check projection and ring position along the spine before changing physical dimensions.
2. **Give the ring its rounded, layered cross-section.** Current ring reads as a narrow, hard-edged polygonal rectangular belt. Target has rounded/chamfered shoulders, visible inner and outer surface depth, broad recessed dark radiator fields, segmented collars, and armor bands. Add those large form changes before increasing tiny window count. Match projected ring thickness using cross-section shape while respecting the stated overall/floor diameter contract.
3. **Rebuild the hub and central spine.** Target hub is a substantial engineered bearing with layered dark rims and amber nodes, joining an armored modular spine. Current hub is a smooth barrel intersected by plain black tubing. Add rotating collar hierarchy, armored modules, copper pipes and the visible equipment clusters that make the structure read as a ship.
4. **Thicken and articulate spokes.** Current spokes are thin straight rods with minimal lattice and bright dashes. Target spokes have substantial rectangular truss depth, diagonal braces, layered rails, central orange conduits and large ring/hub attachment shoes. Increase their visual depth and joint size instead of only adding lights.
5. **Upgrade the shared cockpit, pods and dish.** Command module needs the target multi-pane wraparound canopy, broader chamfered shell and external equipment; pods need stepped dark endcaps. Dish should read as a concave antenna on a substantial articulated mount, not a thin pale disc. Preserve the target ochre/cream language but split broad flat color bands with structural trim, radiators and restrained wear.

## Shared priority

These renders have the broad role motifs, but the target language comes from layered medium-scale geometry that is mostly absent. Work order: silhouette/component proportions and framing → bevels, shoulders, collars, recesses and machinery depth → differentiated metal/glass/paint → small wear and lamps. Adding further identical grilles or higher-frequency dirt to the current primitives will not close the main gap. Target backgrounds also have denser fine stars, but that is secondary to ship geometry.
