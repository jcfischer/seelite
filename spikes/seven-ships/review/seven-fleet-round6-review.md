## Interceptor

**Structural progress:** three thicker armored center units and broader double collars make the spine far less skeletal. Cabin and center volume are stronger; teal glass is closer than the earlier pale reflective pane. The visibly broken triangular pod pattern from round 3 is no longer prominent in this hero.

**Largest remaining gaps and exact next changes:**

1. Center still repeats identical cream roof, black flank box, ring, and straight full-length tubing. Target center comprises unequal cylinder assemblies with partially exposed flanks and irregular heavy couplings. Give the three units different axial lengths and cross sections, interrupt the long straight tubing into short local connections, and deepen a few dark component bays. Avoid hiding the entire mechanism behind matching cream roofs.
2. Cabin glass is a giant flat trapezoid with dark triangular sides, projecting broadly over the hull. Target has a compact inset, two-stage cockpit integrated into armored cheek geometry. Reduce its width/height, create distinct upper/lower pane angles, surround it with thick armor, and add the blue upper-hull stripe. Add the target large side intake and armored weapon cradle.
3. Pod ends are black shoulders leading to huge square grille sheets. Build a smaller octagonal/beveled projecting endcap, then recess the grille inside it. Reduce frame span to expose perimeter armor. Rear engine rings need broad metallic lips and nested recesses rather than dark discs with short radial marks.
4. Scene needs stronger local warm work lights and more differentiated metal highlights. Current lamps are flat cream chips; target lamps have bright centers, amber edges and nearby illumination. Preserve shadow darkness while increasing grazing highlights on collars.

## Patrol

**Progress:** foundations improve gun support; a central raised rail/equipment run makes the dorsal surface busier. The cockpit is darker and less mirror-like.

**Largest remaining gaps and exact next changes:**

1. Main hull is still a long thick rectangular slab with abrupt side-cheek terminations and a broad blunt face. Target has a continuous staged, tapered forward hull, broader around the canopy and a shorter rounded snout. Redraw cross-section stations as one connected armored volume; taper the side shoulders into the forebody rather than ending them as separate flat-faced blocks.
2. Central terrace and nacelle joins need actual open machinery channels. Lower the joining deck; put deep, dark longitudinal channels between central spine and side masses. Fill selected portions with unequal cylindrical couplings, ribbed hoses and recessed equipment. A straight tube along a solid wall is not the target channel.
3. Replace the enormous rectangular gun plinths with chamfered hull-integrated shoulders. Shape gun bodies with sloped armor, breech sleeves, narrowed muzzles and mechanical mount hardware. Current large square blocks worsen toy-like scale despite resolving attachment.
4. Dome is a polished unsegmented hemisphere with a continuous glowing equator. Target has armor sectors, a dark window band, local cyan indicators and a substantial circular mounting collar. Divide the dome accordingly and reduce the uniform cyan ring.
5. Nose stripe must follow the hull (attachment issue above). Target blue pod markings wrap broad hull bands; current markings remain little vertical tags. Paint several actual adjoining armor panels as one coherent band, preserving seams.

## Raider

**Progress:** gun mount bases now read as supported; longer barrels are more legible. The central rail assembly adds silhouette detail. These changes leave the principal silhouette almost unchanged from round 3.

**Largest remaining gaps and exact next changes:**

1. Rebuild the forward cross-section into three distinct longitudinal masses separated by deep, dark machinery channels. Target guns sit along the flank structures and are immersed in couplings/hoses; current guns stand high on broad plinths on a terraced slab. Lower their mounts into the flank structures and extend segmented barrels forward with collars, breeches and local supports.
2. Target is graphite-dominant, with selective worn red and cream patches. Render is red-dominant with a regular red/cream/gray checkerboard. Assign colors to larger structural groups: graphite body/shoulders, a few broad red band regions, fewer cream replacement plates. Break the evenly randomized grid; add larger-scale edge wear and irregular chipped boundaries to those groups.
3. Center rail still spans the length like a railing instead of forming local mechanisms. Remove the continuous upper rail and replace it with short bent pipes crossing the newly excavated channels at selected stations.
4. Cabin has the same large unsupported-looking triangular side-glass shape as the other roles. Target is a narrow, heavily armored raised two-tier cabin. Build the solid cab first, inset separate forward planes of glass, and place thick side frames around them.
5. Replace protruding nose strip as described above. Target tip is a low beveled intake/light assembly, not the current red polygonal plug. Rebuild the terminal face with recessed grille, dark rim and grouped corner lamps.

## Prospector

**Structural progress:** thicker cream center bodies now make a machinery chain rather than a thin barred tube. The larger-looking cabin and teal glass help. This warrants a moderate improvement, but its role-defining cabin/drill architecture is still missing.

**Largest remaining gaps and exact next changes:**

1. Target has a compact, full-depth chain of distinct industrial modules; current ship remains a long narrow central cylinder with matching broad collars. Shorten exposed gaps, deepen side mechanisms, replace the repeated three center units with unequal rectangular chamfered housings, piston couplings and short connector bundles. Mid pod faces need actual piston/collar interfaces instead of blank yellow octagons.
2. Separate a tall boxy occupied upper cabin from its low armored chassis. Current cabin remains a single interceptor-like wedge. Add two-stage forward glazing in the upper cabin and strong side cheeks/lower chassis. This is a silhouette change, not another glass material adjustment.
3. Drill remains a blunt multi-cylinder nose despite additional toothed rings. Target tool projects substantially farther ahead and ends in a tapered pointed stack. Lengthen the exposed cutter and use descending conical stages terminating at a point, a larger toothed chuck and several separated cradle claws. Current broad bottom runner reads as a skid rather than articulated drill bracing.
4. Sensor mast is a plain black cube with a luminous square. Add the target slim antenna, armored pale instrument housing, side boxes and a visible mechanical base.
5. Hero ship remains too small, particularly in height, and sits low compared with target. Increase projected scale roughly 15–20% and recenter after correcting drill length; preserve margins around the actual drill endpoint.

## Shared material and lighting gap

Targets rely on shaped armor castings, distinct roughness ranges, localized recess darkness, warm utility lighting and visible machinery at middle scale. Current ships still combine uniformly tiled skins with identical little hatches and smooth dark fittings. Panel lines/rivets are too uniformly distributed; large armor areas lack meaningful subdivision by function. Glass looks like a teal opaque sheet without target interior cues or varied reflections. Implement strong structural hierarchy first, then use paint wear concentrated at edges, larger irregular scuffs, brushed exposed metal on rims/collars, darker recessed bays and locally warm emissive lamps. Increase the sparse background star density to match targets, but it is secondary to the ships.

Next useful review should show changed silhouettes and major assembly relationships in flat gray before another detail pass. It should also include a shallow side view across patrol/raider gun foundations and nose strips so that actual support surfaces can be inspected directly.
