Ring reaches the user 7/10 stopping threshold. Drum and Assembly do not. Assembly regresses despite a better docking surround: added service pods visibly float, and its end module has become a box rather than the target octagonal pod.

## Ring

The larger opening, dark projecting surround, interrupted window belt and raised transverse edge strips produce a modest but real improvement. The main silhouette, four-spoke topology and warm industrial palette are now sufficient for 7/10. This remains a substantial distance from a close match; the threshold should not be read as 90-percent fidelity.

Remaining gaps, in priority order:

1. Illuminate the docking interior walls and floor with a localized amber source. The view still terminates in a harsh dark triangle and a flat pale ramp. The target has a coherent enclosed chamber with lit side panels, ceiling ribs and a detailed landing floor.
2. Thicken the spoke collars and add dark nested flange layers, especially where each spoke meets the ring. Current collars are much lighter and smaller than the reference machinery joints.
3. Build actual broad raised ribs around the torus section. The new silver outlines improve readability, but resemble thin wires against the target deeply layered transverse structures.
4. Reduce the nearly uniform cream-panel brightness and add stronger directional shadow separation. The target hub has more dark framing between smaller irregular armor plates, and the rim has much stronger light-to-shadow variation.

## Drum

The dark front shoulder and four-way endcap hierarchy are closer to the target. Pipe additions help the barrel. These gains are mostly offset by the new docking opening: it is visibly an empty sequence of frames with black side gaps and little warm illumination. It resembles an unfinished tunnel rather than the target lit bay.

Remaining gaps, in priority order:

1. Close and light the docking chamber. Give it continuous interior wall and ceiling surfaces behind the frame, a visible floor with warm reflected light, and amber jamb fixtures. Remove the visible black vertical gaps between the repeated pale internal frames. The large bright white corner lights currently compete with the dim opening.
2. Change the camera/axis projection: the reference barrel runs nearly horizontally across the image and has a flatter front endcap; this model still rises markedly to the right with a tall dominant front disk. Existing geometry detail cannot compensate for this composition mismatch.
3. Make the front shoulder visibly faceted with large dark armored segments and layered seams. The current dark annular bevel is an improvement in color, but remains a smooth repeated band.
4. Put pipe bundles and warm window boxes inside axial machinery troughs with raised sidewalls. The central broad black lane remains largely empty above the fittings, and the top lanes still read as fittings mounted on pale sheets.
5. Replace the sparse endcap vent arrays with continuous structural cross members carrying several pipe diameters and mounting brackets. The reference cross visually connects the rim to the docking surround.

## Assembly

The widened hangar and dark face fittings are improvements. The new silhouette changes introduce more conspicuous defects than they solve: the upper-left short service pod is clearly disconnected above its support truss; the new lower-right short pod is also visibly detached from the intended service span. The terminal module now reads as a chamfered rectangular crate, not the target enlarged octagonal capsule. These are composition regressions and prevent a higher score.

Remaining gaps, in priority order:

1. Seat both new horizontal service pods directly on their trusses. The upper-left pod should lie along the existing horizontal link between the left tank and hub, not above it. Place the lower short pod on the lower truss between hub and hanging tank, not beyond and below the tank. Verify actual silhouette contact in this hero view.
2. Rebuild the terminal as an unmistakable eight-sided axial pod with a broad octagonal end face and strongly chamfered corners. Keep the dark connecting neck, but replace its coarse pale flanges with thinner metal collars. The present square end face is farther from the target than round 3.
3. Lengthen and slim the middle right module, and carry the hanging tank farther outboard. The target has a longer articulated chain; the current large rounded middle barrel compresses its hierarchy.
4. Add equipment junction boxes and localized amber lamps at several heights on the main mast. It remains an uninterrupted skeletal truss, unlike the segmented, serviced target mast.
5. Replace the gold central squares on radiator panels with gold perimeter edges and four or five narrower dark fins. This discrepancy is unchanged.
6. Light the hangar interior as an enclosed warm space. The broader aperture reveals more of the same unconvincing dark triangle and bare ramp seen on Ring.

## Shared observation

The starfield is still markedly sparser and more uniform than all three targets. This is secondary to the disconnected Assembly parts and unfinished Drum chamber. Repeated cream mottling supplies wear, but cannot substitute for the targets varied dark metal construction, stronger local highlights and integrated machinery recesses.
