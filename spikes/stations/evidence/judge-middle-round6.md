## Wheel

The larger hub gives the centre appreciably more mass and makes the mouth a smaller opening within an actual octagonal body. Its visible right side also has more volume. This improves the hub-to-wheel proportion toward the reference and earns a composition increment. It is still an enlarged, fairly flat-faced box rather than the reference's layered barrel, but the massing improvement is real. Lighting, ring structure, surface treatment and detail hierarchy are substantially unchanged, so those categories do not rise.

Remaining priorities:

1. Give the hub stepped collars and separate dark mechanical bands behind its front face. Avoid merely widening the pale flat border again; the target's depth comes from successive volumes.
2. Replace protruding pale ring clips with integrated broad segment joints. Add alternating substantial cover panels to the inner/rear truss to establish the reference's secondary inhabited ring.
3. Bring the vertical spoke equipment into restrained midtones and add localized warm fixtures. Its machinery remains much darker and harder to read than the target.
4. Break uniform small dirt flecks into plate-scale value changes, chipped edges and seam staining.

## Buoy

Round 6 adds warm illumination across the upper hatch and adjacent hull. This makes the hatch more readable, but the conspicuous localized amber patch on the otherwise flat hatch reads as a spotlight and does not yet recover the reference's smaller fixture-led accents and layered mechanical shadows. It also emphasizes the broad pale hatch border. The useful local change is insufficient to raise the whole lighting category at the established tenth-point precision; the score remains 6.9. No material or structural improvement is visible relative to round 5.

Remaining priorities:

1. Replace the isolated pale pointed roof supports with continuous dark diagonal braces visibly joining roof framing to body piers. Add curved service pipes beside the front/right pier; these are major missing mid-scale structures.
2. Make the hatch a recessed, chamfered mechanical assembly with compact top lighting and shorter side/bottom lights. Distribute warm illumination from those visible fixtures rather than a dominant patch on the hatch face.
3. Rotate the lower radiator to expose its ribbed face and give it a substantial dark border and hinge root. Its current edge-on wedge remains a clear silhouette mismatch.
4. Layer the roof perimeter, interrupt the pristine pad markings, lower and widen the hut, and reduce the four oversized luminous antenna tips.
5. Introduce equipment recesses and varied plates on the broad right cylinder surface; concentrate wear at seams rather than adding more evenly distributed noise.

The sparse starfield remains a secondary shared gap. Further generic vent boxes or overall brightness would not resolve the structural differences above.
