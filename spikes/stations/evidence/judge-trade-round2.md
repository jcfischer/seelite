## Ring

The hub is now larger relative to the ring and its clipped corners, side structure, spoke machinery and perimeter rails create useful depth. The improved material score reflects stronger separation between armor and attached dark machinery. Framing has moved farther away, however: the target ring nearly fills the frame, whereas the current ring has appreciably wider top and side margins. The actual ring still reads as a flat-faced annular wall, not the rounded, heavily armored torus in the target. Its exterior dressing remains largely a repeating alternation of grille and small window units.

Highest-impact corrections:

1. Change the ring section itself. Use several broad bevel faces around its cross-section so the front slopes into inner and outer rims. Preserve visible armor breadth, with a deep dark recessed circumferential service/window lane through the pale plating. Thin rails laid onto the flat face cannot achieve this.
2. Build fewer but much heavier transverse structural ribs wrapping across that section, with layered collar assemblies at the four spokes. The target uses broad dark metal structure and multi-part joints; the current fine rails and cuffs feel lightweight.
3. Increase docking aperture width and give it a thick projecting surround, lit amber jambs and visible warm floor/walls. The current near-black small opening loses the target focal point. Add layered roof equipment and front corner armor rather than leaving the hub face as broad plain octagonal wedges.
4. Move the camera moderately closer to restore the target occupancy, and increase amber light presence around spoke collars and ring channels. Current small near-white lights scarcely affect nearby surfaces.

## Drum

The crop is fixed: the full station is now visible with margins. Smaller radial service runs are a clear improvement over round 1’s giant front grilles. Additional rib brackets and barrel fixtures add depth. But the target is a horizontally substantial cylinder with a deep, faceted armored rim; the current view reads as a relatively tall end disk, a plain circular perimeter, and a long barrel whose broad black strip is still mostly empty. The target has multiple dense recessed axial machinery troughs.

Highest-impact corrections:

1. Replace the narrow, round-looking front border with a broad faceted bevel of dark metal armor, an inset pale endcap, and a second outer collar. Target perimeter construction occupies a meaningful fraction of the front radius. The current concentric thin bands cannot carry that mass.
2. Make the docking port roughly a third wider in the image and illuminate its interior amber. Build two or three nested surround layers, hazard strips and jamb lights; the port currently remains too small and almost black.
3. Populate axial barrel channels with visible pipe bundles, cylinders, rails and amber window groups. Break pale plating into raised armor sections separated by those troughs. More grilles attached to the middle of otherwise plain sheets are not equivalent.
4. Move toward the target camera/silhouette: the target has a broader horizontal footprint and less tall projected front disk. After geometry changes, slightly reduce camera elevation and tune perspective/orientation against the reference rather than only changing distance.

## Assembly

The octagonal hub silhouette is a worthwhile change. Additional service fittings, right-tube bands and the small left tie truss help. The station is now smaller in the frame than round 1, although the target is much wider and fills substantially more of both axes. The right extension remains a nearly continuous pale tube; its terminal piece has not become the target’s distinct enlarged faceted pod. The left tank remains visually continuous. The upper radiator is still on the opposite side of the mast from the target.

Highest-impact corrections:

1. Rebuild the right extension as visibly different sections: short broad hub adapter, narrower exposed dark connector, armored long module, another narrow connector, then enlarged octagonal terminal pod. Break its silhouette and material rhythm. Surface bands on the current uniform tube do not solve this.
2. Widen the assembly and increase image occupancy. The reference has a broad horizontal span with a substantial hub; the implementation is a small central cluster inside excessive black margins. Tune camera only after the missing extension and auxiliary structure exist.
3. Split the left cylinder into independently collared upper/lower tanks linked through a transverse hub connector. Add the upper-left and lower-right horizontal service modules carried by trusses; these are important intermediate-scale silhouettes in the target.
4. Move the upper radiator to the mast’s left, create separate narrow fins with gold end frames, and add occasional mast service boxes/amber lamps. Current radiators still read as broad dark slabs.
5. Deepen the docking surround and brighten its interior amber. The octagonal front remains predominantly plain pale panels; target machinery crosses and layered surrounds give it much stronger relief.

## Shared diagnosis

The new small mechanical attachments help, but the remaining gap is not a shortage of isolated greebles. It is the target’s structural hierarchy: broad beveled volumes, heavy dark frame members, recessed machinery lanes, and warm illuminated docking apertures. The existing weathered armor texture is serviceable. Prioritize those larger-scale forms and local illumination before spending another round on vents or fine rails. The target starfield is also much denser, but remains secondary to the stations themselves.
