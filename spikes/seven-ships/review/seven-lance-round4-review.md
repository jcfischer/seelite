## Largest actionable differences, in order

1. **Rebuild the cockpit opening and glazing as one coherent inset assembly.** The target has a compact elevated dark frame carrying blue glass over several changing planes, with a continuous surrounding shell and distinct smaller forward pane. Round 4 has an isolated rectangular roof opening and a separate lower windshield with large black triangular fins projecting at both sides. These read as intersecting/floating geometry, not a canopy. The cabin roof between the two openings becomes a broad blank bridge. Build a continuous chamfered opening, inset the blue panes within its perimeter, and verify the shell does not cut through or hide the glazing. Preserve the target smaller glazing area without splitting it into unrelated holes.

2. **Give the three spine assemblies their actual radial layers and unequal equipment occupancy.** The new spine is more substantial and less ladder-like, but now reads as a nearly continuous long white polygonal tube with identical black hoops and repeated side boxes. The target has distinct armored cylinder sections, thick multi-part bearing collars, dark recessed annuli, bracket feet, attached asymmetric equipment housings and bundles of mixed-diameter pipes occupying the gaps. Replace the paired smooth hose-like hoops with broad sectional structural collars; leave shorter exposed connector regions between differing armor modules. Current long flat top panels and straight orange rod emphasize repetition rather than the target dense machinery. Do this before adding small bolts.

3. **Nacelle front geometry still has the wrong hierarchy.** Round 4 adds a large nearly black shoulder, but keeps a tall rectangular grille frame standing on its face. The target shoulder remains an armored gray chamfered transition surrounding a smaller graphite projecting endcap, with another beveled perimeter and nested grille. Shrink the grille footprint, model the stepped endcap thickness and its perimeter bevel, and keep the shoulder legible as several shaded surfaces rather than one black block. The current dark shoulder plus oversize framed grille makes these ends read like boxes with radiators attached.

4. **Restore command-module silhouette and side mechanics.** The target cabin is fuller vertically, rounded/chamfered around its underside and narrows through a more articulated snout. Current cabin is a broad flat-sided wedge with an abrupt rectangular terminal face. The target side carries large dark integrated machinery cases, layered conduits, a deep vertical intake and blue armor markings. Current side is mostly bare tiled beige hull with two thin rails. Build the intake recess and substantial attached machinery into the cabin silhouette; shape the nose stations and lower edge; add the large blue roof/side armor regions as part of that layout.

5. **Rear engine rims need broad metallic lips and internal depth.** The current visible aft rims remain narrow dark barrel/disc forms with repeated light dashes. The target rims have a wide rounded metallic lip, separate nested inner edge, a dark interior and warm light in the inner recess. Their broad highlights are a major rear silhouette cue. Model the lip cross-section rather than decorating a dark disc.

## Composition and geometry

- Framing is closer, but target nearly fills the width with the rear engine lip close to the left edge; current image leaves considerably more left margin. Match bounding-box and key landmarks before increasing global zoom: the current nose is already close to the right edge.
- The target rear pod cluster spreads farther left and feels more massive around the central axis. The current cluster is more tightly stacked and its extreme aft extent is too far right relative to the nose. Adjust projected pod reach/spread and cabin-to-pod relationship, rather than simply scaling the entire ship.
- The target engine-to-cabin axis is slightly flatter in the frame. The current lower-right nose and upper pod make a steeper diagonal. A small camera/orientation correction should be judged against matched component landmarks, not guessed independently.
- Large pod mounts remain thin, mostly hidden connector shapes. The target has a substantial diagonal armored support on the upper pod and crowded mechanical connections around the rear hub. Give these supports visible width and depth.
- Some sharp black triangular patches remain on the lower pod shoulders/junctions. They are less visibly striped than round 3, but still unlike the target. Inspect normals, intersections and material boundaries before attributing every black polygon to intentional shadow.

## Lighting

No justified lighting-score increase. The target has stronger directional metallic highlights on edges, rich dark recesses and warm lamps with local glow. Current broad armor is evenly beige, with limited difference between sloped and vertical panel faces; machinery collapses into black shapes or reads as smooth dark plastic. Use lighting and material response together to reveal collar/endcap depth. Keep shadow cavities dark while retaining highlights on their lips. Lamps are mostly pale flat slivers; the target has brighter orange-white cores, amber surrounds and illumination at nearby fixture edges. The backdrop also has substantially fewer visible stars than the target.

## Materials

- Armor remains a regular tiled grid with uniform small dark flecks. Target uses larger irregular plate divisions, localized chipped edges, directional wear and more tonal variation between panels. Reduce repetitive grid dominance, especially across the cabin and tube tops.
- Target metal pipes and collar edges have distinct roughness and metallic highlights. Current black rings are smooth uniform bands; the orange longitudinal rail is a flat solid color. Separate paint, oxidized warm metal, dark steel and bright worn edges.
- Blue glass hue is improved from the prior silver windshield, but flat cyan panes plus malformed frame geometry do not produce the target deep reflective glazing.
- Gray-blue painted panels on the cabin remain absent; thin blue pod strips do not substitute for those major target color regions.

## Details

Missing target-specific details include clustered collar fasteners/bracket feet, layered pod-end surfaces, deep cabin side intake, articulated nose grille/headlamp housings, medium-size side equipment cases and varied connector hardware. Existing top vents, identical rail fittings and uniform panel seams cannot carry those roles. Details score drops because the malformed canopy is a conspicuous new defect in the principal focal module.

Fix the canopy geometry first, then author one complete nacelle end and one complete spine collar/module to the target cross-section and material hierarchy. Reuse only after those assemblies visually match. Another pass of regular surface fittings or extra thin pipes will not close the remaining gap. This round gains only 0.1 overall; it does not establish successful architectural convergence or justify an approval claim.
