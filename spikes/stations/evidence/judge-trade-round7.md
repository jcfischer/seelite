## Drum: shell rebuild is a net regression

The continuous endcap cross is improved: slender rails now reach between rim and bay, and the smaller bay surround avoids the former oversized white corner lamps. The side panel subdivisions are clearer. Those gains are outweighed by the disappearance of most visible machinery. Broad axial strips now read as empty black lanes bounded by simple rails; the round 6 pipes, cylinders, grilles, and equipment supplied valuable depth and scale. The target has dense, varied machinery inside these lanes. New seams alone cannot replace it.

The rebuild also leaves a larger uninterrupted pale endcap with radial wedge seams. The target organizes this face as substantial rectangular armor plates around a broad, layered equipment cross. The bevel remains pale in large sectors and lacks the target's segmented dark armor. Uniform coarse mottling now dominates more of the image, reducing the separation between plate, frame, and working equipment. Silhouette and viewing angle remain recognizable, but surface organization is less faithful overall.

Priorities:

1. Restore dense visible machinery within the axial recesses: cylinders of different diameters, pipe bundles, clamps, small grilles and lit equipment faces. Keep the new panel organization, but make these lanes read as occupied service spaces.
2. Widen and layer the endcap cross; divide the pale face into rectangular armor fields rather than conspicuous radial wedges.
3. Darken the broad front bevel and segment it with seams, small plates and rim fixtures. The target's dark framing is a major visual mass.
4. Give the bay a clearly recessed ceiling, receding wall joints and smaller distributed amber lights. It still reads as a shallow bright box with a dominant illuminated left wall.
5. Introduce sharper local metal highlights and differing wear scales. The pale shell and dark structure currently share too much diffuse, mottled response.

## Assembly: small, visible gains across three categories

The radiator redesign is a clear improvement: narrow dark fins and gold edge lines replace the conspicuous center gold blocks. The hanging tank moves right and the unwanted truss extension is removed, giving the lower service span more legibility. Amber lamps now make the mast junctions visible as serviced structures. These changes justify +0.1 composition, +0.1 lighting, and +0.1 detail. Materials remain unchanged in quality. The score is a narrow pass, not close reproduction.

Remaining priorities:

1. Rebuild the terminal's broad visible pale faces around dark perimeter armor and substantial cross straps. Current vents and thin seams still leave it looking like a plain barrel beside the target's layered capsule.
2. Articulate and space the axial module chain. The target separates collars, necks and service modules clearly; the current pale chain remains comparatively merged and coarse.
3. Lift the lower horizontal service pod into the connecting truss span. It is more exposed now but hangs below the span, whereas the target integrates it along that line. Expose the upper service span more clearly too.
4. Refine the hangar ceiling and far wall. The conspicuous diagonal interior division is reduced, but the target's room depth and coherent surfaces are still stronger.
5. Refine mast junction housings and radiator proportions; the lamps and new fins are progress, while the housings remain simple cubes and the upper radiator is too tall and narrow relative to the target.

Both captures still have a much sparser, more uniform starfield than their targets and lack tiny independent scale cues. These remain secondary to the station structures above.
