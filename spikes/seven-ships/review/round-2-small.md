There is improvement in framing, cleaner paint and added structure. None is close to target fidelity yet. The shared builder vocabulary still produces uniform boxes, simple extrusions and repeated fittings where the targets have purpose-shaped modules and layered mechanical assemblies.

## Interceptor

**Improved:** larger frame occupancy, less blotchy paint, occupied central spine, additional cockpit-side equipment. The overall four-pod motif is clear.

**Repeated architectural blockers, highest priority first:**

1. **Spine hierarchy and proportions.** Target has a thick sequence of distinct armored cylindrical modules with substantial dark collars and locally exposed machinery. Render still has a long narrow rail chassis with seven nearly identical flat box attachments. Replace this repeated bay system with two or three larger modules having different profiles, meaningful joints and clustered equipment. Increase spine depth and end-module mass relative to its length.
2. **Pods need a new endcap/shoulder construction.** Same round-1 vent-box silhouette remains. Current front grille occupies nearly the entire flat end and all sides repeat the same three grilles. Target has broad armor shoulders around a smaller projecting graphite snout, stepped chamfers and deep recesses; side equipment is localized. More surface plates on the present box will not solve it.
3. **Canopy is larger but wrong in form.** Current huge pale reflective windshield dominates a simple wedge and stops abruptly at its lower edge. Target has a narrower, darker, multi-plane canopy nested into a fuller command module, continuing into forward glazing. Build that housing and framed inset arrangement rather than scaling the shared flat windshield further.
4. **Rendered module assembly lacks the target shadow hierarchy.** Target has dark mechanical interiors, rounded metallic highlights and grounded amber fixtures. Current mostly front-lit beige planes remain visually flat; thin pipes cannot create the depth that broad recesses and overlapping armor should create. Restore blue command-module accents and distinguish graphite endcaps from machinery metal.

## Patrol

**Improved:** main body is thicker, beak less extreme, top has a stepped deck, framing is closer and coarse dirt is reduced. This is a useful structural improvement.

**Repeated architectural blockers:**

1. **The body is still a long solid extrusion.** Target nose tapers through several shoulder stages into a short blunt end, with a raised narrow canopy integrated into the armor. Current nose is a long uniformly tiled wedge with a large plain end face. Reshape stations along the hull length: broad midbody, upper shoulder transition, canopy recess, narrower lower forward hull and a shorter terminal cap. Surface panels cannot stand in for these changes.
2. **Expose real side machinery channels.** Target nacelles are separated from the core by deep, occupied channels with multiple pipes, collars and large equipment blocks. Current side is mostly a blank continuous wall with two straight rods and one cylinder. Build the channel as a separate structural zone; use actual setback and layered parts, not appendages across a flat surface.
3. **Pod issue is unchanged from interceptor.** Flat full-face grilles and serial side grilles still dominate. Shared nacelle architecture needs substantial bevel shoulders, deep projecting endcaps, wrapped blue armor bands and enlarged engine rims.
4. **Dome and guns are symbols, not assemblies.** Metallic dome material is cleaner but now reads as a shiny smooth hemisphere with a luminous cyan ring. Target has segmented armor, a broad dark equatorial glass band and sparse small lights. Guns need beveled housings, inset barrels and a mounting structure; current plain slabs remain especially visible against the broad empty deck.
5. **Palette/layout:** target has a strong blue dorsal/nose stripe and a dense but composed mix of armor, vents and graphite mechanisms. Render lacks that nose stripe, has vast repetitive tiled cream areas and almost no amber fixture hierarchy. Fix paint zoning and large equipment placement before adding fine scratches.

## Explorer

**Improved:** full ring is now inside the image, paint is cleaner, collars/radiator patches are visible and central spine has equipment. Preserve the 80 m floor / 84 m overall diameter / 74 m length requirements.

**Repeated architectural blockers:**

1. **The ring fits; the axial ship is still far too small in projection.** Target engine-to-nose assembly spans most of the image width, with a large aft engine group visibly extending left of the ring. Render axial assembly spans roughly half that width and its aft engines hide inside the ring silhouette. Establish target landmark ratios for ring, hub, engine tips and nose, then solve camera azimuth plus axial module placement/mass within the mandated dimensions. Do not shrink the ring to conceal this mismatch.
2. **Ring cross-section remains too thin and uniform in appearance.** Target shows a broad rounded armored section with substantial shoulder planes and large inset dark radiator fields. Current looks like a smooth narrow hoop tiled uniformly, with tiny grille stickers. Rebuild the cross-section profile and its axial depth while respecting diameter constraints, then allocate long recessed service/radiator sectors rather than equally spaced small grilles.
3. **Spokes and hub need a structural rebuild, not more lamps.** Target spokes are broad deep trusses carrying paired conduits, diagonal bracing and large footings. Current spokes still read as thin parallel rods with luminous dashes. Target hub is a multi-layer bearing assembly; current is a smooth central barrel and one small rim. Make the joint and spoke cross-sections substantial and differentiated.
4. **Shared spine/cockpit/pod architecture constrains this ship too.** Repeated flat boxes on thin tubing cannot reproduce the target armored cylindrical machinery spine. Canopy is a large simple reflective pane; target is a small multi-plane blue window assembly embedded in a full command module. Fix shared module vocabulary once, but allow explorer-specific proportions and equipment.
5. **Depth and color:** target has warm metal/pipes, amber recess lights and mixed dark/glazed ring windows, with highlights separating layered parts. Render remains mostly uniformly lit pale paint and charcoal lines. Materials improved from mottled dirt but have not gained the target layered material response.

## Architectural next pass

The next pass should change reusable module construction rather than add more identical details:

- A profiled armored module with several longitudinal cross-sections, real shoulders and stepped inset endcaps.
- A machinery bay that combines substantial overlapping armor, exposed curved conduits, deep collars and locally attached equipment; avoid equal-spaced copies along the entire spine.
- A canopy built from several planes integrated into its hull opening, with consistent dark blue glass and substantial framing.
- An articulated ring cross-section, broad truss spokes and bearing hub respecting explorer dimensions.

Use large silhouette and component-ratio comparisons first. Cleaner textures and larger windshield planes do not remove the repeated geometry blockers. Lighting scores remain unchanged because the new screenshots still lack the target directional depth, strong dark recesses and controlled metallic highlights.
