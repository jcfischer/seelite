# Map #1 constraints

The constraints of the machine map, [jcfischer/seelite#1](https://github.com/jcfischer/seelite/issues/1). They moved here from the map's body on 2026-11-18, when the body reached the tracker's size limit (the-metafactory/soma#1175). They bind every answer on map #1, and through it on map #460: an option that violates one is not on the table. Add or change a constraint here, by PR.

- Browser-only: TypeScript + Vite + Three.js, static bundle, no backend, no runtime network requests (the seekolous delivery model).
- **Counterscale pageviews are the one exception, by the principal's ruling (#569, 2026-09-28).** *"I just want to see how many people are playing, so counterscale on public pages and launch is enough."* A production build loads the principal's self-hosted Counterscale tracker once per public page load, once per game launch and — by the principal's ask on #714 (2026-10-06) — once per opened postcard link, under three site ids; a postcard's recipe rides the fragment, which the tracker never reads, so only `/` and the referrer are sent. Nothing the game does after launch reaches the network, and no dev server, probe or capture loads it. `src/visit-count.ts` owns the mechanism, `tests/public-site.test.ts` allows exactly that module to reach the network, and the Trust page says what is sent and recorded.
- No on-foot / FPS gameplay — the player is always the ship (pre-Odyssey Elite).
- **A neural voice is the one trained asset, by the principal's ruling (2026-10-01).** On asking the player at game start, the game may download the Kokoro-82M speech model (Apache-2.0, about 160 MB with its runtime) **from its own bundle** and keep it in the browser's storage, and generate speech with it at runtime: *"fallback sounds good. Setting sounds good (and a question when the game starts if they want the download)"*. Speech stays generated, never recorded; the weights are the one asset trained on recordings, the files are same-origin (no request leaves the bundle's origin), and a player who declines, or whose browser cannot run it, hears the formant voice. eSpeak (GPL-3.0) runs only at build time to make the lexicon's IPA; only its output ships. The node that ships it owns the mechanism; a settings row names what is stored and deletes it. Imagegen textures embedded in an authored GLB are a separate, later exception: see Rulings, 2026-11-08.
- **Open-licensed instrument samples are allowed for the music, by the principal's ruling (2026-10-06).** *"I am willing to make an exception for the music - as they contribute much to the atmosphere"*, chosen as *"Open-licensed (CC0/CC BY) instrument samples are allowed for music, same-origin, loaded on demand."* Recorded instrument samples — a piano, a harpsichord — under CC0 or CC BY (or a grant as free, such as the University of Iowa's *"without restriction"*) may ship as same-origin files of the bundle — prepared by `scripts/fetch-music-samples.mjs` and **tracked in Git LFS** (the principal, 2026-10-06: *"can't we prepare the samples and GIT LFS them?"*), the deploy pulling them only when it publishes — and loaded only when a song needs them. CC BY sets carry their credit where the game names its sources. Non-commercial (NC) and share-alike (SA) sets stay out. Everything else in the music stays synthesised, and the node that ships a sampled voice owns its mechanism, its size and its credits.
- **The home trailer reaches YouTube only on a press, by the principal's ruling (2026-10-03).** Asked to embed the trailer beside the home title, the principal chose click-to-play over a plain embed and over a link. The home page draws its own poster — a YouTube thumbnail would be a request on load — and only pressing it builds a `youtube-nocookie.com` player; until then the home page asks nothing of another origin. The Trust page names the exception, and `scripts/probe-public-site.mjs` holds that no request leaves the origin before the press.
- **A score is source, and the bundle is not a network request (#543).** A public-domain work hand-encoded as note data in TypeScript and synthesised at runtime meets "no downloaded assets": nothing is downloaded or recorded. And a same-origin file of the static bundle — the music `AudioWorklet` module that `addModule` loads, like every other chunk — is the bundle, not a "runtime network request".
- **Authored models are same-origin bundle files, and only the boot set blocks a start (#830, #835, principal 2026-10-09).** #830 removed "no downloaded assets" for authored GLB models; like #543's worklet, a same-origin model file is the bundle, not a runtime network request. Only the cockpit and the home station's exterior load before a `Game` is built, behind the Retry panel. Every other model installs after boot: the procedural form stands in, the install rebuilds it once, a visible swap is accepted anywhere, a jump never waits, and a failed background load keeps the stand-in. A postcard waits for every model its frame draws. Probes and view sets get a settled predicate on `__seelite` and a URL flag that boots with the full set; the mechanism is #838's. Imagegen textures embedded in an authored GLB are a separate, later exception: see Rulings, 2026-11-08.
- **What counts as public domain here, and where it may be quoted (principal, 2026-09-30).** A work is usable when its composer died more than 70 years ago (Switzerland/EU: before 1956 as of 2026) **and** it was published before 1931 (US: 95 years). The notes are read from an **old edition** — IMSLP's per-country PD tag on the file, never a modern edition's editorial layer — and the score is cited where it is encoded, as #547 cites IMSLP #17764. Such a work may be played as a set-piece **and quoted in the generative bed's fragments**: #548 D12's "original material" rule is relaxed to exclude only *protected* works, so the principal's references (Elite Dangerous, Vangelis, No Man's Sky) still give nothing but manner. A CC-licensed engraving (Mutopia and the like) is a cross-check, never a source to copy.
- Deterministic worlds: the same seed always produces the same galaxy; per-system RNG streams (seekolous `rng.ts` doctrine — one shared stream couples every system to every other's draw count).
- Reuse from seekolous is copy-in-and-diverge: no shared package, no dependency or coupling back to seekolous.
- Toolchain: bun for headless tests, npm-compatible lockfile, no Python.
- No budget for paid services; deploy target is a free static host (Cloudflare Pages, like seekolous).
- **A browser probe's renderer is a choice now, and the default is the player's GPU (#435).** `npm run probe` is `probe:gpu` — `SEELITE_PROBE_RENDERER=hardware` — and `launchChromium` (`scripts/probe-guard.mjs`) refuses to start unless a fresh WebGL canvas reports a non-software renderer, as does every child probe. `npm run probe:cpu` is the deliberate SwiftShader fallback for a displayless or portability check. **The probes still do not run in CI at all (#14)**, so nothing on GitHub renders either way. This line read *"every browser probe in this repo renders through SwiftShader, in CI and locally alike"* until 2026-09-21, and three of its consequences survive the change: a frame cost read off a probe is still a relative signal and must say which lane it was measured on — in flight at 1440x900 the same scene ran 16.8 fps on SwiftShader and 371 fps on an M1 Pro, which is the size of the error a mislabelled figure carries; a threshold tuned on one lane can fail on the other for load rather than for the defect (#99); and a pixel tolerance widened to cross both lanes is a weakened gate wherever it lands (`probe-live-cockpit`'s went 3 → 32 in #435). Any answer that prices a renderer change must say which lane it measured (#98; independently reached at `planet-classes.glsl.ts:394-401` for #59's withdrawn crater band).
- **A probe band on absolute display codes is a property of the subject, not of the lighting (#294).** `probe-light-rig`’s painted-hull band and floors were calibrated on the hull finish that shipped when they were written; one commit changing `DEFAULT_HULL_FINISH` took both rows red and they stayed red for a fortnight, reading exactly like an under-lit hull. `scripts/probe-light-rig.mjs` owns the figures and the calibration they were derived from — they are not restated here. Two rules follow. **Any node that changes a default finish moves every absolute-luma row measured on it**, so it owes a re-derivation rather than a threshold nudge. And **a threshold moved to make a probe green owes a bisect, not a plausible cause**: the move that greened these rows blamed a Metal/SwiftShader readback difference, and the two lanes in fact agree on the peak to two decimals. The rows that survived the subject change untouched were the ones asserting a *relationship* between two readings rather than a code.
- Three flight regimes exist permanently — flight assist on, flight assist off (#5), and cruise (#10). Combat, docking, AI ships and any autopilot must each work under all three; none is a display option, they are different dynamics. Cruise nose-locks velocity and the assist toggle is not in force inside it — Newtonian handling at ~10⁶× is unflyable, not merely hard.
- The HUD is load-bearing, not decoration (#5). Cockpit is the default view, so the hull — which was the attitude readout — is hidden, and the chosen flight model is one where the nose and the velocity genuinely disagree. Any HUD spec must carry a velocity-vector (prograde/retrograde) marker and an attitude reference, or the chosen model is unflyable in the chosen view.
- Nothing is crossable at combat speeds (#6). Combat speed is 2 km/s (#5's assist cap); crossing even the entire near layer (`NEAR_FAR_KM`, 20,000 km) in a minute needs 333 km/s, already 167× that. In-system travel (#10) must supply a cruise or hop regime whatever it chooses. At the astronomical scale #6 settled on, a 60 s crossing of a typical 1.3 AU leg needs ~10⁶× combat speed — about 10 c — so the cruise regime is necessarily superluminal unless the system scale shrinks. (Corrected 2026-08-18: #6's receipt said ~1,000×, computed against a placeholder rather than the measured 2 km/s cap.)
- Systems are multi-destination (#6): 1–3 typed stations, generated from population and tech level. Markets price per *station*, not per system (#7); in-system travel is a routing problem, not a single crossing (#10); docking must answer for each station type (#9).
- The sim has a game clock (#6). Body and station positions are `f(systemSeed, gameTime)`, not constants, and the save carries the clock. Anything time-varying — market regeneration, legal-status decay — reads it rather than inventing its own.
- Sim time is never compressed (#10). Cruise is fictional FTL, not a time-scale hack: orbits, markets and NPCs tick at real time throughout. Anything that wants to speed the world up rather than the ship must argue against this, because the #6 game clock is authoritative for body positions.
- The drive charge is a second consumable (#10), separate from the hyperspace tank: the flown hull's `chargeAu` of cruise range — 12 AU on the courier, 10–40 across the roster (#185) — 1 unit per AU, free refill on docking, and passive regeneration at ~1 AU/min. The regeneration is the anti-stranding rule and is not optional — without it, running dry between stations is an unwinnable save. The HUD must show it.
- **Reachability is a fact about the flown hull; connectivity is a fact about the galaxy (#185).** A hull's `tankLy` is its jump range and its fuel capacity, one number, and a short tank flies a fragmented graph on purpose: at 6 LY every galaxy measured fragments (5.5% of systems cut off), at 5 LY 37.6% are (N = 24 galaxies, seeds 1/47/1234). `JUMP_RANGE_LY = 7` stays galaxy-owned as `layoutGalaxy`'s connectivity predicate — the reach at which every galaxy is connected, which is the courier's — and is nobody's jump gate. Moving it is a migration (#107; #89 has shipped). So anything that says *in range*, *reachable* or *systems within N LY* must say whose hull, and nothing may assume a system reachable by one hull is reachable by another. The one trap is a purchase: a shipyard refuses a hull with zero reach from its system. #554 wired the rest and built that refusal as a law (`hullReachFrom`, `src/hull-reach.ts`), and the Shipyard tab reads it (#583): `hullOffer` refuses a hull with zero reach on its row and prints every other row's reach from here.
- Galaxies are a namespace, not a regeneration (#6). All 8 exist from the start and switching changes one index, so per-galaxy state (visited flags, market fluctuation bytes, legal status) must be saved separately rather than globally.
- A body's render layer is its **distance**, not its kind (#15). Within `HANDOFF_KM` (10,000 km) of the camera it is drawn at 1:1 in the near layer, beyond it at `FAR_SCALE` in the far layer, with a 5% hysteresis band. The scaffold's fixed split — planets far, stations near — draws nothing once the content is real, because a station orbiting a planet an AU away is fifty thousand times past the near camera's far plane. Anything that adds a drawn object (docking geometry, NPC ships, weapons fire) inherits this rule rather than picking a layer.
- **A per-type modifier on a bounded index gets clamped away where that type lives (#13).** Three times in one node: an economy offset died for 46.5% of shipyards because high TL requires low economy; a `government` offset died across the whole rim band where fences concentrate; a factor on the hostile *probability* died from government 4 down. Express a per-type modifier where it cannot saturate — a delta on the price, a factor on the odds — and measure the collapse rate before believing an index shift works. `scripts/measure-clamp-bite.ts` is the instrument.
- **Station types are differentiated by law and equipment, not by price (#13).** The commodity table is unscoped and type only shifts prices, so §4's exclusives and §5's law carry the whole of #6's not-interchangeable requirement. Weakening either re-opens #6's question, and the spec says so where it can be read before the edit.
- **Legal status is mandatory and belongs to #8 (#13).** The map already presumed it ("legal-status decay" below), but no node specified it. #13's contraband fence and shipyard scan make it load-bearing rather than optional.
- **Illegal goods are the one scoped commodity class (#13).** Everything else trades everywhere; Narcotics, Firearms and Slaves trade only at a fence. #7 owes a per-commodity legality flag and a visible market marker, or the exception becomes the unread-screen tax the unscoped table exists to avoid.
- **Vocabulary (#13): a *station* is any dockable object, a *target* is any cruise endpoint, a system with several stations is *multi-station*.** "Destination" and "outpost" are retired; `docs/specs/station-types.md` §1 holds the audit grep. Closed nodes keep the old words — they are records, not documents to maintain.
- **One system in ~700 generates unreachable (#13, open as #23).** No planet, so no station and no arrival body. `galaxy-generation.md` §6.4's "always" was false. Until #23 lands, nothing may assume every system is dockable or has an arrival body.
- **The market remembers the player, and that is the first world state not derivable from the seed (#7).** Base prices stay `f(stationSeed, floor(t / 30 min))`, but a sparse per-station **stock deficit** — saved, per galaxy, decaying on a 20-minute half-life — records what the player bought and sold. Anything that reasons about "the market" must ask which half it means.
- **The stock deficit is load-bearing, not tuning (#7).** A shipyard→refinery pair inside one system opens at 28 Cr/t on a 68-second leg. Only the deficit stops that being farmed: it closes any pair whose sell/buy ratio is under 1.920, and bounds the rest at 158 Cr a run against an interstellar route's 291 Cr saturated. Removing or weakening it re-opens single-system farming as the dominant strategy.
- **A per-run comparison is not a per-good one (#7).** Gold, Platinum and Gem-Stones skip the hold check, so a run carries a full hold **and** all three. Any claim about what a cargo is worth must price the stack, not the best single commodity — doing the latter inverted #7's contraband finding.
- **Contraband does not currently pay (#7 §4.1, open in #8).** The best legal run is 3,746 Cr against smuggling's 2,314 — a 62% deficit, structural because Narcotics' positive gradient wants the opposite leg from the negative-gradient metals. #13 §5.2's rim-weighted fence geography has no economic basis until #8 prices the risk or the economy is retuned.
- **Fuel is 0.2 Cr per 0.1 LY — 14 Cr a full tank (#7 §6.1).** `galaxy-generation.md` and `in-system-travel.md` all carried `0.2 Cr / LY`, a tenth of Elite's PRXS figure, in three places. Corrected; the check is a repo-wide grep.
- **`tsconfig.json` covers `scripts/` (#7).** It did not, so `npx tsc --noEmit` — one of the four gates — never looked at the measurement scripts. Two bugs shipped through that hole in one review, including an instrument printing NaN across every row while the suite stayed green. A script is not exempt from the gate.
- **A measurement instrument must fail loudly (#7).** Every figure `scripts/measure-market.ts` prints goes through a guard that throws on a non-finite value. An instrument that quietly reports garbage is worse than one that dies, because its numbers get transcribed into a spec.
- **Do not write `Resolves #<node>` in a PR body (#7).** GitHub auto-closes the linked issue on merge, which races `soma graph close` and leaves the node closed with no receipt — `soma graph audit` then reports it, and the repair is a reopen. Reference the node without a closing keyword.
- **The HUD must carry a docking alignment cue (#9).** Cockpit is the default view and the hull is hidden, so check 2's axis alignment and check 4's roll match have *no visual referent at all* without an instrument — the same argument #5 made for the velocity-vector marker. Docking's tolerances were set wide (§3.2) partly because the instruments do not exist; a HUD spec that omits both cues leaves them permanently wide.
- **Clearance is a precondition, not a check (#9).** Docking without a live grant is a failed entry *and* turns the station hostile, and the entry condition is `grantLive && !hostile` in that order — because the flip and a hostility check resolve at the same instant, and a read-before-flip implementation would let barging dock cleanly. Anything that reaches a station — the docking computer, NPCs, any later autopilot — goes through the protocol rather than around it.
- **A drawn opening is not a hole until the surface is cut, and the check that catches it measures *in* the plane (#155, open as #162).** All twelve forms had a solid surface across the aperture — the trade Coriolis's `buildCuboctahedron` +Z square as two triangles, every other form's `hullLoft` front cap — and none of it was visible from inside, because a hull is `FrontSide`. The corridor row skips everything at or behind the plane by construction (`WALL_EPSILON_KM`), so it was green throughout and cannot go red on this class. Everything *behind* the first surface is occluded by §13.1's five-slab `DoubleSide` bay, so the cut is one surface per form — keyed on **that surface** and never on `aperturePlaneKm`, because the Coriolis's frame stands 25 m proud of its own hull face (#128's 6%) while the other eleven sit flush. The cut edge is the aperture rectangle exactly: the four bars lie strictly outside it, so a larger cut shows bay through the gap and a smaller one re-closes the hole.
- **Two ratified artifacts disagree about which way a ship is rolled (#157).** Check 4 (`docking.md` §3) measures the roll as `|dot(shipUp, basis.across)|`, so its zero puts a hull's beam along the slot's *short* axis; `tests/aperture-fit.test.ts` measures `w·cosθ + h·sinθ` against the width with `w` the beam, so its zero puts the beam along the *long* one — and the 120 x 70 aperture was ratified from that measurement. They have disagreed by a right angle since #9 and #35. Nothing compares the two zeros, which is why it took flying it.
- **A station's aperture is a hole only if something draws one (#147).** Every form frames its opening with a slab spanning it, and that read as an opening only while a dark `BackSide` recess was drawn in front of it. §13's bay replaced the recess, and the slab was left across the mouth — from inside, the way out was a lit grey panel, and no rim on the bay's own bulkhead helped because the rim was behind it. The trade Coriolis's coaming is four bars now; the other forms' equivalents have not been looked at from inside. Anything that draws behind an aperture inherits this.
- **A grant *is* a berth reservation (#147, `docking.md` §13.2).** Clearance names the berth the ship will park in, queues when no free berth fits, denies a hull no berth fits at all, is spent on the outward crossing, and is not evicted by its own expiry while a ship is parked on it. Anything that reaches a station — the docking computer, NPC traffic, any later autopilot — goes through this rather than around it.
- **Nothing headless can see geometry poking through a hull (#147).** A hull mesh is `FrontSide`, so a ray cast from inside passes straight through and reports nothing, and a box over a whole station is satisfied by any interior at all — a 400 m cap passed it. `tests/station-interior.test.ts` measures the bay against the *slot* forms' own `core`; the two pad roles' bays are held by the canonical view set alone. A cap invented for this class was added on a misdiagnosis and deleted a round later.
- **A distribution corpus is sized by its rarest binding bucket, never by the total (#28).** `station-types.md` §9.1 asked for the fence rates "over ≥ 20,000 refineries", but the assertion that binds is the rate at `government = 7` — 735 of 156,388 refineries over 200 worlds, two orders of magnitude rarer than `g = 0`. At 20,000 total that bucket holds ~95, where the standard error is wider than the band it is asked to be inside, so the test passes or fails on its seed set. Any verification obligation over a distribution must name the N *of the bucket the claim rests on*, and say which half is gated in `npm test` and which is an instrument run by hand — a band checked only by a script no suite invokes is a band nothing checks.
- **A station's display reach and its dockable geometry are separate tables, in separate modules (#28).** `STATION_GEOMETRY` (`station.ts`) is what #9's docking checks read; `STATION_SILHOUETTE_KM` (`station-mesh.ts`) is how far the drawn object reaches — the drydock frame, the asteroid, the flare stacks. §5's "the frame is silhouette, not dockable geometry" is then a fact about imports rather than a comment inside the struct it forbids. Both are budgets a test enforces: no vertex of a built station may lie outside its row, because a number nothing measures is a number that drifts — the frame shipped at 2.0 km against a declared 1.5.
- **An angular cone is not a containment test (#9).** A 35° alignment cone at a 0.6 km station admits ~0.42 km of lateral offset against a 0.20 km slot, so the checks as first written let a ship dock through the hull face. Any geometry gate that admits by angle owes a separate containment row, and the failure is invisible until something measures where the crossing point actually landed.
- **A clearance around a body must be scaled to what orbits it, not to the body (#30).** `SURFACE_MARGIN` cleared the ship by 5% of a *planet's* radius while `system.ts` puts a station 400–2,000 km above one, so a big enough world projected a shell its own station was inside: 36 of the 249 legs whose station was in clear space ended against the host anyway, thousands of km short at thousands of km/s. The body a cruise target orbits now guards at an **absolute** `radius + CLEARANCE_KM`; everything else keeps the proportional shell. Any later rule that puts a keep-out volume around a body — weapons range, interdiction, an NPC's collision avoidance — inherits the question, and the check is whether the volume can grow past the thing it is meant to let you reach.
- **A ray that starts inside a sphere has no entry point, and every sweep that forgets it pins the ship (#30).** `sphereEntry` returns 0 for a step beginning within the sphere, which reads as "stop here" — so 49 of 337 stations, all of them gas giants, sat inside their host's shell and were places cruise could not be engaged from at all, whichever way the ship turned. The release test is the forward ray's **closest approach**, not "is the centre ahead of me": from inside, a surface subtends *less* than a hemisphere, so a hemisphere test frees the radial heading and pins every heading around the limb. Anything that sweeps a volume — docking clearance, interdiction bubbles, weapons — owes the inside-the-volume case its own answer.
- **A quarter of all legs to a planet-orbiting station are occluded by its planet (#30, open as #32).** 26.1% have the host between ship and station at leg start, and no straight-line rule reaches those. The rate is a function of `r/R` alone, so it is orbital altitude and not the guard — 22.9% on rocky worlds against **41.2% on gas giants**, whose stations sit at r/R 1.026. Until #32 lands, nothing may assume a selected station is reachable in a straight line.
- **A classification measured at leg start is not a fact about the flown leg (#30).** Bodies move: two legs classified occluded arrive anyway, because over a ~70 s crossing the station comes out from behind its planet — the line that clipped the host by 7 km at the start ends 132 km clear. Anything that decides reachability, line of sight or a firing solution from one snapshot of the world clock is answering a different question from the one the player experiences.
- **The guard is a snapshot, and bodies move (#30, open as #37).** Every sweep in `cruise.ts` tests a ray against where the masses are at the top of the frame, so a body on a 30 km/s orbit moves ~3 km inside one — enough that a heading released with 3 km of clearance is a kilometre inside it by the next test. Measured at 1 leg in 90. Anything that inherits the sweep — docking clearance, interdiction, weapons fire — inherits the still photograph with it.
- **A metric that floors at zero cannot verify a rule about clearance (#30, PR #33).** "Deepest penetration 0.000 km" read identically for a ship stopped 5 km clear and one standing on a surface, and the build with the defect printed it too; a median then hid a leg at −1.041 km, and a minimum was dominated by that one leg on both builds. What discriminated was the *count* of legs ending on a surface: 43 of 90 against 1 of 90. Before offering a figure as verification, check it separates the fixed build from the broken one.

- **The capital classes do not dock, and that is a decision rather than a gap (#56).** Measured against the roster #35 built: the carrier is 212 × 181 m and the dreadnought 186 × 165 m, against a 200 × 70 m slot and the refinery's 150 m pad. Neither fits any aperture in the game and neither is meant to — they are a separate class, ratified 2026-08-26. So #26's roster owes them an outfitting route that is not a station, and nothing may quietly widen an aperture to admit them. `tests/aperture-fit.test.ts` holds both halves: every ordinary hull fits every aperture (tightest 23 m, the miner through a slot), and neither capital fits anything.
- **The slot is 120 × 70 m, and its binding dimension is its height (#56).** `docking.md` §5's provisional 200 × 70 was 3.7× the widest ordinary hull's beam and 16% of a 1.24 km station's whole width, against about 10% on the reference Coriolis — a hangar door rather than a slot. Ratified 2026-08-26 against #35's measured roster: 66 m of width margin against 13 m of height — both **rolled** figures, measured at the 50° roll limit, not raw subtractions. **This line read 120 × 60 until #26 checked it (2026-09-01).** `station.ts` declares `heightKm: 0.07` on both slot roles and #56's own gist says *"the docking slot is 120x70 measured at the 50° roll limit"*; at 60 the miner's rolled section does not fit at all, and `tests/aperture-fit.test.ts` asserts every ordinary hull fits every aperture. A wrong figure here costs a hull designer 10 m they have. The height being the dimension §3 wants tight. A retune that widens the slot is answering a question nothing asked, and one that widens it far enough to admit a capital reverses the constraint above.

- **An emissive surface tinted by a generated colour must not take its exposure from that colour (#59).** The star shader multiplies its tint by up to 1.52, tuned in the gallery against one warm hue. A *saturated* tint clips one channel and keeps the others, so its detail survives; a *neutral* tint clips all three together and the disc is a flat white lamp — `0xf8f7ff` measured 235 with a granulation spread of 2.8 against a warm star's 199 and 4.5. `PHOTOSPHERE_LUMA` normalises every tint to one exposure, so the tint carries hue and not brightness: a star's apparent brightness on screen is already a rendering choice, and what reads as class is the colour. Anything else that tints an emissive by a generated colour inherits the question, and the tell is whether the *neutral* end of the palette still has detail. The light a star **casts** is a separate question and is deliberately not normalised.
- **A cross-body comparison cannot verify that a per-kind rule decides the pixels (#59).** Seed decoration *within* a kind is as large as the difference *between* kinds: two gas giants sit 38.3 apart on a four-number descriptor, against a closest cross-class gap of 29.3 — so a build that threw the nine class shaders away and drew everything with one seed-parameterised shader would look exactly as varied and pass. What works is drawing the **same subject under each kind in turn**, holding seed, light, camera and geometry still; all eight classes then separate at 40× the same-class repeat and a one-shader build fails flat. Three checks were built and withdrawn before that one landed, two of them because a constant had been tuned until the verdict came out. Any later identity check — ship hulls, station roles, weapon signatures — owes the same question: could this pass on a build that ignored the thing it is checking?
- **A measurement's floor is a maximum, so a floor taken over too few subjects inflates the ratio in the passing direction (#59).** Got wrong twice in one node — once by sampling three of six bodies, once by taking a repeat floor from the only class with no time-varying term. And a swept constant must be swept on *both* sides of the comparison it scales: sweeping the gap while leaving the floor fixed produced a clean 20/20 that was pure bias, inside the sweep added to catch exactly that.

- **A comparison must offer both sides the same menu (#25).** The greedy-trader
  measurement was wrong four times before it was right, and three of the faults
  are one shape: the clock, the option set, or the price table differed between
  the two arms being compared, and each difference flattered the arm the claim
  was about. Flights were priced at departure while waits were priced at the
  epoch they waited for; the wait branch was costed against intra-system
  follow-ons only while the flights it lost to included jumps; the wait was
  priced past the epoch boundary and executed onto it. The measured tail went
  1.326× → 1.785× → 4.679× as each rigging came out. The check is not "is the
  arithmetic right" but **could the losing side have been given what the winning
  side had** — and the tell is that every correction moved the answer in the
  same direction.
- **A `>` threshold is not a measurement (#25).** Two figures were invented in

- **A rule that lives only in an instrument is not shipped (#25).** §4's

- **Market state is `f(stationSeed, epoch)` plus a saved deficit, and the
  station's identity is a carrier key rather than its `id` (#25).**
  `Station.localCarrierId` and `LOCAL_CARRIER_ID` are the persistence table

  `carrierKey` is the only way to make one, and `parseDeficits` checks the shape

- **The economy is faster than §8 aimed for, and §5.5's "waiting never beats
  flying" is false (#25).** The greedy ceiling covers the full 19,125 Cr kit in
  48–85 minutes against a 1.5–2.5 h target, so that target survives only as a
  claim about a human at ~0.6× the ceiling. Waiting for a market re-roll beat
  flying at 2 of 143 decision points, by up to 4.679×, **at every dock cost in

- **There is no second UI grammar: every screen lives in the cockpit (#70).** Docked is not a mode outside `hud-and-scanner.md` §1's language — flight is suspended (#73's E5) but the player is still in the cockpit, and the market lives on a *station console* the forward wall carries while docked. A separate full-screen mode was on the table and declined. So any later screen — a galaxy map, a mission board, a shipyard — answers *which wall or which tab*, never *which application*, and a proposal for a second grammar is re-opening this rather than extending it. **Out-of-fiction surfaces are the exception, and they are not a second grammar (#304):** settings and the Commander overlay are plain-DOM overlays over the game, because they are neither station business nor fiction — game business still takes a wall or a tab.

- **The galaxy map's chart cannot also be its finder (#11).** In a freely
  orbited 256-system cluster, the share of systems whose nearest on-screen
  neighbour falls inside the 14 px pick radius is **40.2% at the framing that
  fits the whole cluster**, and never falls below about 9% — reaching that only
  at 14 LY, where a third of the galaxy is off screen. There is no camera
  distance that both frames the cluster and lets you pick, and no point-size
  tuning changes it: it is 256 points in a ball whose core is three times as
  dense as its rim. So a **filterable Navigation list is load-bearing rather
  than a convenience** (#69 owes it), and any later spatial selection surface —
  a contact list, a mission board's map, a target picker — owes the same
  question: can the thing be *found* on it, or only read? `galaxy-map.md` §5.1
  holds the table and `probe-galaxy-map.mjs` asserts the default-framing figure
  per viewport.

- **Persist the intent, derive the projection (#11).** A set course saves a
  *destination*, not a route chain. `persistent-world-state.md` §8 forbids
  persisting a derivable projection, and a saved chain has a second failure the
  spec need not argue: it describes a position the ship has left, so the first
  off-plan jump desyncs it. Re-deriving from where the ship actually is makes
  that jump a re-plan instead. Anything else that wants to remember a plan —
  a trade route, a patrol, a queued upgrade — inherits the distinction.

- **A grep for prose cannot check a number (#11).** `src/galaxy.ts` carried
  `FUEL_COST_PER_LY = 0.2` for six weeks after #7 corrected exactly that
  transcription in the specs, because the correction's stated check was a
  repo-wide grep for `0.2 Cr` — which finds text, and the code held a bare
  literal on a differently-named constant while `market.ts` held the right
  figure under another name. When two modules name one quantity, the check is an

  bug survives. And a claim that nothing was mispriced is only as strong as the
  searches behind it, which is why `galaxy-map.md` §4.1 states the method rather
  than the conclusion.

- **The galaxy is a `q = 0.70` disc, and `t` is not the distance from the centre (#118).** `systemPosition` scales its `y` term by `GALAXY_FLATTENING_Q`, so the cluster reaches `R = 24.53` LY across and `0.7 R` through — and §2's reference plane is now real geography rather than an arbitrary convention. Two quantities that used to be one are now separate: `radiusLy` is the plain Euclidean distance and is what "most central" means, while **every radial gradient in §4 reads the *profile* radius** `√(x² + (y/q)² + z²)`, the coordinate §3's density profile is written on. Because the profile's core radius is `R/√2`, that coordinate is scale-free in `x/R` — so re-deriving the radius moved every position and **no system's polity**. Anything that adds a radial rule inherits the distinction, and reading it off the Euclidean distance is the change that looks harmless: it compresses the gradient toward the core for polar systems and drops the core:rim ratio below the 3.0 §3 quotes.

- **A prediction presented as a measurement survives for years (#118).** `galaxy-generation.md` §2.1 quoted **8.25 systems in range** as the figure the radius was solved for, and #107 carried it forward into a new derivation. It is the output of a *uniform-ball* closed form; the generator is 3:1 concentrated and rejects fragmented layouts, and the ball that actually shipped held **7.88**. Nobody had measured it because the closed form was in the spec beside the constant, reading like a result. So: a figure a spec states as measured owes a **mode in a shipped instrument** that reprints it — `measure-galaxy-shape.ts`'s `density` and `grid` exist because a table headed "measured" and a claim about "every viewing angle" had no way to be rechecked. And the sibling rule, from the same node: **a per-galaxy figure quoted as a constant hides its spread.** §5.1's 40.2% is one galaxy of a 13-point range, and a probe band set from it was already outside its own subject at galaxy 4. Band from the spread, and state the `N`.

- **A fix is the next defect often enough to plan for it (#118).** Six review rounds, and three blockers were caused by the previous round's fix: a probe restating `=== 22` was replaced by a 15–40 bracket that no shippable shape could fail; parameterising a bisection deleted the assertion its test was named for; hoisting a normaliser into a function put a second `atan` on the generator's hot path. The one that matters most is procedural — **an edit that does not land reads exactly like one that did**, because the suite goes green either way when nothing under test covers the claim. Grep the file for the new text before reporting a prose change done.

- **A check that clicks the centre cannot find a boundary bug (#11).** Three

- **A snapshot's integrity hash must cover what arrived, not what parsing made
  of it (#46).** Hashing the re-canonicalised model made every difference
  canonicalisation smooths away invisible to the check: it agreed with itself by
  construction, and a save with its carrier maps in another key order passed
  while the returned model differed from the bytes. The cost of hashing the
  received body is that the check can no longer say *which* difference it found

  And it is a **check, not a guarantee**: a 32-bit FNV-1a catches an accidental

- **Two modules that name one quantity owe an assertion that they agree — and

- **An angle taken from a projection into a plane is confident where it is
  meaningless, and two implementations of it will not agree there (#69).** With

- **An instrument for a check must read the check's own arithmetic, and the

- **A renderer's own record cannot prove that anything was painted (#69).** A

- **A line written to `lastExit` was never said to anyone (#447).** Its fifteen

- **A check that asks whether the geometry is right never asks whether it is

- **A wide tolerance cannot be drawn at its true angular size (#94).** A 35°

- **An instrument anchored to the world answers a question a glass one cannot

- **A per-station instrument's worst case is not always where its design puts it

- **A pixel check needs an arm that registers hits (#94).** The clip check went

- **The lighting environment reaches `MeshStandardMaterial` and nothing else

- **There is a `quality` key now, and every later expensive visual term declares

- **A probe threshold that encodes a frame rate fails on load rather than on the

- **Changing the galaxy's shape changes what every system *is*, so it must
  precede save/load (#107).** `systemFromPosition` derives economy, government

  the shipyard at system 42 attaches to a system that is now agricultural with no
  shipyard. #118's reshape is free only while no save exists. Once #89 ships,

- **A generated surface's *layout* decides what it reads as, before any of its content does (#101).** Ship plating shipped twice as a square grid of identical plates with a groove four texels either side of a sixteen-texel pitch, and was flown and called *scales*. Both faults are geometric and neither is subtle once the map is looked at rather than the frame: a wall of identical squares is tiling, and a bevel a quarter of the way across a plate on all four sides domes it into a pillow — a 25 cm chamfer on a one-metre plate where a real seam is millimetres. What plating is, is strips four times longer than wide in a **brick bond**, with the seam a scribed line. Anything later that generates a repeating surface — #105's cockpit, #102's greebles, a planet's cities — owes the same three questions: aspect, bond, and what fraction of a cell the seam occupies. And the check is to render the *map* and look at it, because the frame is where the fault is least visible.
- **There is one surface generator, and a new consumer declares a profile rather than adding one (#101).** `src/surface.ts` makes the plate, wear and seam-normal texels for stations, ship hulls and — when #105 lands — the cockpit, keyed by a `PlatingProfile` and a hash tag, never by a type. It carries the layout as well as the content, so the station roles state the square grid they already had instead of inheriting it, and four checksum pins prove those figures reproduce #36's buffers byte for byte. A second generator would be the same arithmetic written twice, then three times.
- **A hull's edge wear cannot come from a map, and a hull's finish cannot come from the profile (#101).** No texture in UV space has been told which plates meet, so edges come from geometry: `hullLoft` writes a per-vertex `crease` attribute and the material patches it into `roughnessFactor`. And a station has one finish per role where a hull has four, so `MeshStandardMaterial`'s scalar is a per-material dial over one shared curve — forcing it to 1 so the map is "the whole answer" repaints every paint on the hull to whichever one the clean plate happens to read.
- **`probe-guard` cannot tell two sibling worktrees apart, and every visual capture is exposed until #123 lands (#101).** It fingerprints a module by the top-level numeric constants present in both the served text and the local file, and two branches off one commit agree on every one unless a branch changes a module constant. The tell was arithmetic — a view that could not have moved had moved, and one that must have had not. Until #123 lands, a capture's serving tree is checked by hand: `lsof -d cwd` on the listening process, plus a fetch of a module that exists in only one of the two trees.
- **Anything mounted on a form is snapped onto its surface, not authored onto it (#129).** A zone says *where on the station*; a raycast against the form's own geometry says *where exactly*. With the radii written by hand, seven of the twelve station forms floated their clutter — the Gantry worst at 102 m off a keel that tapers from 0.2·r to 0.42·r along its length, the Hangar's slab chamfering at both ends, a twelve-sided flat-shaded drum's surface sitting 3.4% inside the radius its control rings declare.
