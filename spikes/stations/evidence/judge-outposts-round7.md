## Rig

The drill now has a pointed termination, the jaws have additional smaller pivot elements and more articulated end shapes, and the lower brace attachment at the cylinder is more substantial. These are visible detail improvements worth 0.10. The broad arrangement and surface response remain essentially unchanged. The left-projecting diagonal below the pad remains conspicuous; the target has a more convincing enclosed structural connection. This is a narrow pass, not a declaration that the structure is finished.

1. **Resolve brace ends and faces.** The long diagonal still extends left beyond its apparent junction. Shorten it into a fitted pad socket. Plate the broad lower diagonal face, leaving small inset ribs rather than an open trough. Keep the large triangular void.
2. **Integrate the shoulder and crown.** The twin vent faces still form a shelf in front of a broad plain collar. Join their sides into the crown with substantial cheeks and stepped housings. The crown itself remains a mostly blank box with three simple posts, unlike the target’s compact mechanical roof.
3. **Give jaws distinct materials and taper.** The added joints improve articulation, but thick pale angular blocks and dark flat circular pivots still read as a simplified claw. Make the outer jaw shells dark worn metal, retain smaller cream armor patches, rim the pivots and taper the inner teeth. Preserve the newly pointed bit.
4. **Replace front deck grille dominance with recessed pipe banks.** The target front edge reads as one structural beam holding pipes; the current three large hanging grille boxes dominate it. Reduce their depth and span, and fit horizontal pipes into the beam.
5. **Improve broad material and light hierarchy.** Uniform fine grime covers most pale surfaces. Add larger gray abrasion on the pad, darker seams and restrained metallic edge response. The target has deeper recess shadows and localized warm fixture light; increasing whole-scene brightness would worsen the pale-block appearance.

## Salvage

The hanging window rods are repaired: side dividers now sit within the glass and upper glazing has divisions. A new bent lower pipe and offset lower-left module break the former straight-column silhouette slightly. The added tower-base machinery is visible, although its bright lamp attracts more attention than the target’s modest fixtures. These earn 0.05 composition and 0.10 detail. The dominant cockpit wedge, pad treatment and regular cabinet stack remain largely unchanged.

1. **Reshape the cockpit before adding more small parts.** Its huge ivory wedge still dominates the right side. Reduce its visible broad side, step the upper shoulder and add recessed panel breaks; widen the subdued rust-red patch vertically. The target cockpit is one component within a dense assembly, not most of the right silhouette.
2. **Replace pad markings and wear.** The pad still has regular dashed yellow edges, two dark rectangular patches and uniform brown texture. Use broad irregular pale scuffs, chipped continuous boundary paint and varied missing-paint islands. Place small warm fixtures on the upper pad edge itself.
3. **Make the lower mass genuinely asymmetric.** The new pipe helps but reads as an exposed loop around the same straight cabinet column. Offset a substantial lower module in depth and sideways, add a rounded underslung tank, and angle the terminal keel inward. Fit the pipe into these masses with collars rather than using a loop as the silhouette change on its own.
4. **Complete the tower and left corridor.** The tower still lacks the target’s clear diagonal support on its left, while the bright right lattice is visually dominant. Add a dark substantial left brace. Give the left connection thick end frames and real depth, reducing the thin bright crossbars against the plain pod wall.
5. **Preserve the window repair and restrain new lamps.** The glazing now reads cleanly; do not enlarge its bars. Reduce the tower-base lamp’s prominence and distribute small warm pools near actual service fixtures. Broad material separation and shadows will deliver more fidelity than additional glowing squares or repeated vent cabinets.

## Next priority

Salvage: cockpit proportion and stepped mass, then irregular pad abrasion and lower asymmetry. Rig: brace end connection and shoulder integration. Both need broader material variation rather than denser fine grime.
