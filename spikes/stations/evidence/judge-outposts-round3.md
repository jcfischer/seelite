## Rig

The complete assembly fills the frame much better. The enclosed bridge, taller crown, thicker truss members and enlarged hinge discs are useful gains. The deck is now readable and has an inner outline. These do not yet reproduce the target proportions or machinery hierarchy.

1. **Reduce the bare crown and shape its bridge transition.** The target upper housing is narrower than its broad main drum and interrupted by roof machinery, pipes and inset panels. The live crown is a nearly drum-width square tower with huge bare faces and three plain posts. Narrow its upper body, break the front with substantial service equipment, replace the plain posts with varied exhaust stacks, and give the bridge the target sloping, recessed equipment section.
2. **Broaden/shorten the main silo and compact the open span.** The target presents a stout industrial body; live is still a tall narrow cylinder with a long triangular void beneath the deck. Move toward a broader body and reduce the deck-to-body gap, retaining the newly substantial braces. Add flanged beam faces and unequal internal braces instead of uniformly repeated lattice.
3. **Extend the lower mechanism.** The drill is still a small conical nub. The target has a long stepped drill housing below a busy neck and articulated claws with bent terminal jaws. Extend the central mechanism, subdivide it into metallic collars, and add a second claw joint plus darker inset faces. The hinge discs alone do not supply that silhouette.
4. **Separate deck, painted hull and structural metal.** The deck is now almost the same pale dirty material as the hull. Make it medium gray with broad irregular scuffs and a stronger pale landing outline. The crown and bridge also need darker metal borders and recesses to avoid reading as stone blocks. Add localized amber illumination on adjacent structure; current lamps are mostly bright pixels.
5. **Replace repeated side fittings where silhouette matters.** Target side tanks are two tiers of boxlike equipment, whereas live has long slender cylinders. Build the target larger offset pods and the bottom rack before adding further small grilles.

## Cluster

Longer right connectors, clearly separated pods, a reduced front hatch, visible lower mast and closer framing correct several major round2 problems. Rounded pod shoulders also help. The main silhouette is substantially closer, though its left connection and finish remain behind the target.

1. **Expose the left connector.** The left pod still hugs/overlaps the cube corner in this view; target has a clearly visible flanged neck. Move it farther left and slightly toward the camera as needed until a dark gap surrounds the connection. Right connectors now have enough length, but need large dark flanged collars and attached utilities rather than long uniform ivory tubes.
2. **Darken the deck independently.** Target has a gray worn landing surface, thick faded yellow border and broad pale inner outline. Live deck remains near hull color, with clean thin lines. Use a distinct gray deck material, larger scuffed patches and broken edge paint. Add actual perimeter railings and a few upright lamp posts; the corner blocks alone do not match the rooftop silhouette.
3. **Give the masts their equipment.** Upper mast is a coarse four-rail frame ending abruptly, without the target aerial tips, smaller crossmembers, secondary antenna/dish and two roof base units. Lower mast is visible now but short. Extend it and add the fine crossmember rhythm that makes the target read as a communications outpost.
4. **Balance wall lights and machinery.** Large white lamps still dominate the front wall. Reduce their area, warm their color and reveal pipes/corner metal with soft fill and edge highlights. Target hardware has articulated recesses; current black frames/grilles remain comparatively flat and repetitive.
5. **Finish the front equipment layout.** Hatch scale is much closer, but the target also has an offset left service assembly, right pipe bundle and a sculpted underslung pod. Break up the remaining large front wall fields using those specific forms. Maintain the improved ivory-to-dark balance rather than covering every field with identical vents.

## Salvage

The ivory hull with red stripe and angular cockpit is a clear improvement over the orange wedge and floating window blocks. A stepped lower body replaces the orange box, the open tower rim and rear dish appear, and the left module now has some separation. These gains are partially offset by lost surface depth and an over-bright, homogeneous front.

1. **Restore the central armored frame and dark pad.** Target has a heavy black structural perimeter around a thick projecting gray pad. Live is mostly one pale front wall with narrow dark vertical pipes, a pale pad and an immaculate yellow outline. Make the outer beams visibly thick, return the pad to medium/dark gray, bevel its lip, add mounting brackets and chipped broad yellow markings. This is the most important remaining target mismatch.
2. **Make the upper tower visibly supported.** The plain two-band cylinder appears suspended above the roof in the hero, and only a right-side brace is legible. Add substantial paired feet/legs under it and two diagonal supports; expose the machinery base. Retain the open rim but add inset vertical machinery, rim segmentation and inner depth. The large bright dish currently competes with the tower: darken its structural material and place more of it behind the cylinder as in the target.
3. **Refine the cockpit beyond a simplified nose.** Its outline and stripe are much closer, but the glazing remains two large unarticulated dark rectangles. Add frame divisions around wraparound glazing, a raised roof assembly and smaller hull seams/equipment. Restrict brightness so shoulder facets and recesses retain shape.
4. **Make the lower keel asymmetric and hooked.** The new descending blocks taper, but form a straight stack of repeated cabinets. The target lower half bends inward through unequal volumes, exposed pipes and a narrow terminal keel. Offset the intermediate blocks laterally, replace at least one cabinet face with curved pipes/tanks, and angle the final member inward.
5. **Increase connection depth and material diversity.** Left pod still reads as a detached plain box on a few rods; use a short visible lattice corridor with darker interior. Give the right lower box its stepped attachments and underslung tanks. Across the build, differentiate painted hull, exposed metal, pipe highlights and landing plate roughness. Uniform brown speckles on every pale face do not reproduce the target layered wear.

## Priority for another round

Cluster: gray deck/rails, exposed left neck, detailed masts and connector collars. Rig: narrow detailed crown, broader drum, longer drill and articulated claws. Salvage: heavy central frame/dark worn pad, credible tower supports and asymmetric pipe-rich keel. These are more valuable than additional repeated surface cabinets.
