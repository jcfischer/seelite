**None reaches the user threshold of 7/10.** This round makes meaningful structural progress; it is not just another greeble pass. However, the target construction is still appreciably heavier, more layered and less uniformly pale. The unchanged detail scores reflect improved coarse geometry without corresponding improvement in fine construction fidelity.

## Ring

The newly beveled ring section, continuous dark illuminated lane, stronger transverse bands, yellow stripes and larger frame occupancy resolve several major prior complaints. The ring now reads as an armored torus instead of a thin annular wall. The brighter docking rim restores a focal point. This is the closest of the three.

Remaining high-impact gaps:

1. The target hub has a large aperture in a thick projecting dark machinery frame. The current aperture is still small, set within several concentric pale plate borders. Widen the visible aperture and replace some of those pale borders with a deep dark frame carrying jamb machinery and hazard edging. Light the interior walls and floor amber; a bright outline surrounding a dark triangular view does not match the target hangar.
2. Give the transverse ring bands actual stepped thickness and metal edge layers. They currently read largely as broad dark stripes with a single attached vent. Target ribs wrap around the section and carry multiple parallel raised members. Keep the new torus profile.
3. Make spoke collars substantially more massive and multi-layered at the ring and hub. Target collars bridge the joint with several nested rings and service structure; current thin collars leave the joints visually lightweight.
4. Break the continuous window strip into deeper framed groups, with occasional pipe/vent interruptions and localized warm lamps. Current regular bright squares create a smooth decorative belt; the target lane is recessed industrial infrastructure.

## Drum

The endcap bevel is visible, the docking border is brighter, and added barrel fittings/lamps reduce the empty-strip problem. These are improvements, but the principal target mismatch persists: the target has a heavy dark faceted front shoulder and machinery-filled axial troughs, whereas the current model has a broad pale inset collar and broad mostly flat barrel sheets.

Remaining high-impact gaps:

1. Convert the broad front bevel to dark armored metal, with several large faceted panels and a layered outside collar. The current pale bevel makes the front disk even more dominant. The target front rim is dark, thick and structural, enclosing pale quadrants.
2. Reduce the endcap service pattern from eight evenly spaced radial spokes to the target strong four-way cross, and thicken those cross members with pipes and junction brackets. Preserve large pale quadrants between them.
3. Build axial troughs with visible depth: inset black floor, projecting side lips, large pipe/cylinder bundles and amber window boxes. The current rails remain mostly attached to flat surfaces, and the large central dark lane still has appreciable empty area.
4. Widen the hangar aperture and layer its surround. Its new bright continuous outline improves visibility but is less mechanical than the target surround. Illuminate interior sidewall panels and floor, with separated jamb lamps instead of relying on the glowing rectangle.
5. Tune camera elevation and axis projection after these changes. The current station still rises diagonally and presents a tall front disk; the reference is a wider horizontal barrel with a somewhat flatter projected endcap. Distance alone will not solve this.

## Assembly

The split left tanks, distinct enlarged terminal pod, relocated upper radiator, increased occupancy and warmer docking frame are all real improvements. The overall topology is now much closer to the target. The remaining difference is visible in module proportions and in the still-sparse intermediate structure.

Remaining high-impact gaps:

1. Lengthen the right extension and separate its modules with exposed dark connectors. Current connector sections remain pale and the terminal pod is a rounded barrel. The target ends in an unmistakably angular enlarged octagonal pod, reached through a short dark neck; the middle module is longer and slimmer. Match that silhouette before adding surface fittings.
2. Add the visible upper-left and lower-right horizontal service modules on their trusses. The current upper-left support remains essentially empty, while the lower-right tank hangs directly below the main tube. In the target it is outboard, attached through a narrow vertical connector and a lower horizontal service truss. These missing forms account for much of the narrower, simpler appearance.
3. Add periodic equipment boxes and warm lamps along the main mast, especially at radiator junctions. The current bare truss is too uniform. Split radiator faces into four or five narrow fins with gold perimeter edges; current central gold squares on broad dark slabs do not resemble the target treatment.
4. Use a heavy dark cross and projecting hangar surround on the hub face. The current nested pale outlines emphasize a flat panel face, whereas target machinery divides the octagon into armored quadrants. The target opening is wider and reveals a warm panelled interior.
5. Extend horizontal occupancy moderately while preserving full mast visibility. Reference hardware reaches nearly the right edge; the current right pod still leaves a large margin. Geometry changes above should supply much of this width.

## Shared priorities

Retain the structural gains. Next effort should target heavy dark framing, genuinely recessed machinery lanes and visible illuminated docking interiors. Surface weathering is already recognizable, but repeated mottled cream panels dominate too much of each object. Avoid compensating with additional small vents. The target has darker metal with readable edge highlights and more variation in panel size, inset depth and local illumination. The starfield is closer than round 2 but still much sparser than the targets; this remains a lower priority than the stations.
