## Wheel

The larger octagonal hub is a meaningful improvement, and spoke pipe/grille additions replace some blank strips. Its transverse scale is closer to the target. However, the new camera retreats too far: the wheel spans roughly three quarters of image height, while the target nearly fills it. The target ring has a visibly broader, deeper inhabited band, and its hub is a layered barrel with prominent angled fins; live remains a chamfered box. New spoke machinery is mostly black, so the vertical spokes visually collapse into one pale rail with a black neighboring stripe. The mouth is less warmly legible than the target.

Highest-impact changes:

1. Move the camera about 15% closer while retaining full rim margins. Then thicken the ring face radially by roughly 35%, with dark layered edge channels and projecting section joints. Do not solve ring thickness by camera changes.
2. Add axial hub collars and four outward angled fins. Break the broad flat octagonal face into a smaller projecting mouth frame, surrounding inset machinery and segmented plate facets. The target hub has a sequence of nested depths rather than one pale face around one black rectangle.
3. Give spoke machinery a mid-dark metal value so pipe edges remain visible; integrate a broad outer casing and warm recessed light points. Preserve the contrast between casing, recess and individual conduits.
4. Use restrained amber illumination around and within the mouth and along the rear ring. Existing white window rows are too uniform. The sparse nearly invisible starfield still loses the target atmosphere.
5. Material remains predominantly beige mottled plate. Add larger plate-value variation, dark seam-localized grime and selective edge highlights; do not add another global speckle layer.

## Derrick

Deck edge modules, service pipes, tank-side equipment and small warm lamps make the installation more credible. Removing the bright pad-center glow and high-contrast grid is a clear improvement. The entire base is now visible. But framing overshoots in the other direction: the structure is markedly smaller than the target, with excessive top margin. Most seriously, tank caps still consist of flat concentric disks. This conspicuous silhouette mismatch remains from round 1. The pad has become a largely empty dark plane with a thin yellow perimeter, while the target has a worn central landing rectangle and visible plate structure.

Highest-impact changes:

1. Replace flat disk shoulders with domed or smoothly faceted pressure-vessel shoulders, small necks and valve stacks. Current pipe additions do not disguise the lids. Carry pipes from these necks into the central service core using elbows and supports.
2. Enlarge the hero framing by roughly 20%, retaining a margin under the base and above the antenna. Target-like framing will expose whether current small details are sufficient.
3. Deepen the deck fascia and corner guards; current repeated vent rectangles form a thin linear trim. Add vertical brackets crossing the fascia, occasional large inset grilles, under-edge pipes and a raised perimeter service channel.
4. Add a worn central landing rectangle, subtle plate divisions and localized hazard bars. Keep the central glow removed. The target deck is visibly used metal, not an almost featureless dark square.
5. Brighten the central service core enough to reveal layered panels behind X braces; add warm vertical fixtures rather than only scattered dots. Lower deck still has a bare pale plinth edge: add cradles, rail and occasional boxed equipment.
6. Widen and articulate the hut with attached equipment, door, dark framing and multiple thin antennae. The existing glowing-window cube remains a weak match.

## Buoy

The front hatch, vertical pipe bank, lower machinery and raised rear panel establish more of the intended vocabulary. The pad marking and central lighting are improved. Yet the live silhouette is still a tall plain drum beneath a square slab, with repeated horizontal trays. The front tray obscures the very hatch that should be a principal identity feature. The new raised radiator is nearly vertical and narrow, whereas the target has a wider tilted panel with a visible face. The camera is now much too distant: the station occupies only roughly half the image width, compared with most of the target width. This is a composition regression despite improved geometry.

Highest-impact changes:

1. Bring the hero camera roughly 30–35% closer and lower its elevation slightly so hull machinery commands more attention relative to the roof. Retain the full appendage silhouette.
2. Remove or relocate the front mid-height tray. Make the front service hatch broad and strongly layered, with a pale inner frame, dark outer brackets and short amber edge strips. Place the small front radiator below the body and tilt it downward, as in the target.
3. Broaden and tilt the raised rear radiator so its ribbed face is visible, and give it substantial hinge supports. Keep a single strong side radiator; the remaining repeated trays undermine the target asymmetry.
4. Shorten the drum relative to its width and add substantial vertical armored piers plus faceted top shoulders. Connect the square deck to the hull with diagonal brackets. Current pipes are too narrow to supply the target structural weight.
5. Fill the lower belt with a few larger horizontal tanks, framed machinery boxes and dark gaps. Target machinery forms an irregular relief belt; the live lower edge is still a continuous smooth cylinder with additions stuck onto it.
6. Improve illumination of the hatch, pipes and underside of the deck. These new parts are presently very dark and visually fuse together. Use small warm fixtures and edge catchlights, with differentiated metal values, rather than raising every surface uniformly.

## Shared priority

Correct framing and large silhouette mismatches before adding more repeated vents. Retain the useful removal of pad glare and grid markings. The next meaningful quality gain needs domed Derrick tanks, asymmetric Buoy appendages and strong hatch geometry, and a thicker Wheel ring with a layered finned hub. The overall render still needs more readable mid-dark machinery, local warm lighting and a restrained visible starfield. These are not yet threshold-passing images.
