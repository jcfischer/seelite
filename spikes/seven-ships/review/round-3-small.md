**Verdict: modest local improvement, overall convergence has stalled.** The new pod shoulders are a real improvement. However, the screenshots still preserve nearly all major proportional, camera, material and machinery-hierarchy mismatches. A change described as an architectural rebuild does not earn a large score increase unless the visible structure changes enough to match the target.

## Interceptor

Visible gains: pod ends now taper through more substantial shoulders; side vents are localized rather than repeated three times; center section has curved tubes and exposed components; cockpit has another pane division.

Remaining blockers:

1. The target is compact, thick and heavily armored through the spine, with a large command module and massive rear pods. Render retains a long thin center run and relatively small end modules. Measure projected pod width, cabin width and spine thickness against ship length and adjust those ratios first. Camera framing remains smaller than target.
2. Spine is still an almost evenly repeated ladder of black bays and exposed rails. Target has two or three substantial, irregularly occupied armored cylinder assemblies, broad collars and equipment clusters. Enlarging thin pipe loops does not create this hierarchy. Build separate pressure/armor modules with differing lengths and cross-sections, then machinery connecting them.
3. Pod grille remains a framed rectangular sheet at the very front. Target has a smaller graphite projecting endcap with a beveled perimeter and multiple stepped surfaces. Add that depth and reduce grille dominance. Engine rims need a broad metallic lip and nested inner rim, not a nearly featureless dark disc.
4. Cabin remains a plain oversized windshield over a wedge. Target uses blue glass on multiple changing planes, a fuller rounded/chamfered hull and substantial dark side mechanics. Current bright silver glass, missing blue hull marking and bare snout retain the wrong focal appearance.
5. There are conspicuous black/white triangular fan patterns on the upper surfaces of the lower rear pod near the spine. They do not occur in the target and look like a geometry/UV/rendering artifact; inspect before adding detail.

## Patrol

Visible gains: better pod shoulders, localized pod vents, larger gun housings and barrels, extra side blocks/vents. The guns now register clearly.

Remaining blockers:

1. Hull still reads as a long thick tiled slab with a blunt rectangular terminal face. New side blocks create square discontinuities rather than the target smoothly staged armored shoulder. Redesign the hull stations and the forebody silhouette as one connected volume. Target forward third is broader near the cockpit and narrows into a shorter, more rounded end.
2. The central dorsal terrace remains mostly empty flat armor. Target has a shaped raised spine, armored weapon mounts, setbacks, channels and locally recessed equipment. Design those medium-scale volumes before attaching more vents.
3. Main-hull-to-nacelle connection still lacks a deep open machinery channel. A long pipe along a wall and one exposed cylinder are not the target dense nested engine/coolant assemblies. Recess the joining surface and build multiple unequal components inside it.
4. Broad blue nose stripe is still missing. Pod blue markings have become narrow isolated strips rather than the target wrapped bands. These are major color regions and should be authored as part of panel layout.
5. Dome is still a polished hemisphere with a continuous cyan ring; guns are still plain rectangular housings; canopy remains a large simple dark pane. Those iconic forms need sectional geometry, dark glazing bands and proper armored mount shapes. Frame/lighting/material scores remain unchanged.

## Explorer

Visible changes to shared pods and spine are small at the displayed scale. No score increase: ring, axial proportions, spokes, hub and camera—the dominant image features—are essentially unchanged.

Remaining blockers:

1. Target rear engine group visibly projects far beyond the left edge of the ring and command section extends far to the right. Render places nearly the entire short-looking axial assembly within the ring silhouette. This is the largest mismatch. Solve camera azimuth and longitudinal component placement together using ring/hub/nose/engine image landmarks while retaining the 80 m floor, 84 m overall diameter and 74 m length contract.
2. Ring still reads as a narrow uniform hoop. Target has a thick rounded armored cross-section with broad recessed dark radiator regions and substantial collars. Adding small grille patches to the same hoop has not corrected cross-section or depth. Increase appropriate axial thickness/shoulder shaping within the fixed radial limits, and rebuild long sectors as recessed assemblies.
3. Spokes remain extremely thin compared with target deep rectangular trusses. Give them meaningful width, depth, diagonal webbing, internal conduits and large attachment shoes. Current pairs of rods with bright dashes do not convey the target structural mass.
4. Hub remains a plain barrel with a single small rim. Target has multiple coaxial bearing collars, darker recessed rings, large armored joints and warm lamps. This is a central architectural assembly and deserves its own geometry rather than another decorative ring.
5. Target cockpit and engine modules are large enough to communicate detail; current projected size loses it. Fix first-order component/camera relationships before another shared detail pass.

## Shared diagnosis and next action

The remaining gap is not a lack of greebles. Target construction is organized at three scales: a strong role silhouette, a handful of distinct structural assemblies, then localized fittings. Current renders have the role silhouette plus a dense regular skin, with weak or repetitive middle-scale assemblies. Rendered paint also remains uniformly beige and dark-lined, glass too flat/reflective, and machinery lacks the target varied roughness, recess darkness and directional highlights.

For the next pass, compare simple monochrome silhouettes and projected component ratios first. Then rebuild one nacelle, one spine module and one integrated canopy to target depth before propagating them. Explorer needs its own ring/spoke/hub and camera pass. Repeating small shared attachments again is unlikely to improve these scores materially.
