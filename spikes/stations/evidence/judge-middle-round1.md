## Wheel

The ring and four spokes give the right topology. The target has a substantial octagonal/cylindrical hub, thick inhabited ring and integrated box-section spokes; live has a small square tunnel box and thin paired bars. Approximate projected hub width is only ~18% of outer diameter versus ~30% in the target. The ring face is also visibly narrower. Rear inspection confirms that many surfaces are bare strips rather than machinery-bearing structure.

Priority changes:

1. Enlarge the hub by roughly 1.5–1.7x in transverse silhouette, build an octagonal barrel around the required rectangular mouth, add axial collars and the four angled fins. Preserve the clear docking corridor. This is the largest identity mismatch.
2. Thicken the ring's radial band roughly 35–50%, add layered rim lips and deeper recessed dark side channels. Build broad spoke casings with recessed central machinery instead of two isolated pale bars.
3. Add dark joint bands, raised framed vent housings, service pipes and occasional projecting boxes to ring/spokes. Current repeated vent/window alternation is too uniform and sparse.
4. Warm and distribute mounted lights; target has small amber points along the rear ring, spoke channels and mouth. Current white window strips dominate. Add restrained starfield visibility; current background is nearly empty.
5. Reduce broad beige/noisy concrete-like patches and introduce plate-to-plate metal value/roughness variation, edge wear and grime concentrated at joints. Light should catch beveled rims, not merely reveal flat rectangles.

## Derrick

The top deck, tall central support and lower tank cluster match the main arrangement. Live top slab is thinner and much more featureless; the tanks have stacked flat disk lids instead of domed pressure-vessel shoulders. The target's tanks and column belong to one dense service installation; live reads as separate barrels placed on a blank plinth. Hero bottom is cropped at the image edge, unlike the target's comfortable margin.

Priority changes:

1. Replace stepped flat tank caps with rounded shoulder profiles and smaller necks. Add tank-top valve assemblies, pipes running toward the column, base cradles and service walkways so the tank cluster is mechanically connected.
2. Deepen and articulate the top deck fascia with chunky corner guards, inset grilles, vertical bracket modules and exposed lower conduits. Add perimeter rail/service channels. Target deck edge is a major depth cue; live edge is a thin continuous pale wall.
3. Reduce the high-contrast checker/grid marking on the pad. Use one worn landing rectangle and localized hazard bars over subtler dark plate seams. Remove or relocate the conspicuous central glowing source, which has no equivalent in the target.
4. Populate the support with vertical pipes, warm linear lights, ladder and occasional pale panel inserts. Keep X braces visible against a lighter, layered service core rather than a largely black box.
5. Enrich the rear control hut with offset attached equipment, doorway, dark framing, rail and several thin antennae. The current single white-window cube is underdeveloped.
6. Pull camera back enough to retain the entire bottom plate and target-like margin. Use more material separation between tanks, structural steel and deck; the same mottled pale material currently blankets them.

## Buoy

The cylindrical body and top pad match the basic object class, but target is a squat heavily armored faceted service body with asymmetric appendages. Live is a plain tall cylinder under an oversized square slab with four repeated horizontal trays. Target body has a large framed service hatch, corner columns, pipe banks and mounted machinery that determine silhouette and depth; most are absent.

Priority changes:

1. Add the large layered square front service hatch, armored vertical piers and beveled/faceted shoulder transitions. Wrap an irregular lower machinery belt around the body. These large forms matter more than additional surface noise.
2. Reduce the square slab's visual overhang and connect its rim into the hull using brackets, guards and conduits. Current deck looks placed on top of a drum. Build a dense dark rim with pale broken panels to match the target.
3. Replace the symmetric tray arrangement with the target's clear asymmetric radiator vocabulary: one raised tilted rear panel, one substantial side panel and a smaller downward-angled lower panel. Give each panel a thick frame and credible hinge/support depth.
4. Add exposed vertical pipe banks, horizontal tanks, lower service boxes and hatch-edge warm strips. Support rear views with a varied machinery layout rather than repeating identical vents/trays.
5. Make the roof hut wider and lower with offset equipment and multiple antennas. Replace grid-like pad lines with the target's simpler worn landing marking and remove the isolated bright central glow.
6. Break up pale hull plates with dark framing, localized rust at seams, distinct radiator finish and low amber service illumination. Current blanket speckle reads as stone/concrete; target reads as aged assembled metal.

## Shared highest-return work

Prioritize large silhouette and relief geometry, then light placement, then material refinement. Merely increasing noise texture or vent count would leave the major score deductions intact. The targets consistently have heavy structural rims, overlapping plates, restrained warm lights and layered dark recesses; the live models predominantly show thin slabs, broad blank panels and repeated bright window strips.
