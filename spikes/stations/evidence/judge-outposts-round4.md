## Rig

The crown front now has equipment, the deck is gray, side boxes read more industrially, and added lamps help scale. These are useful gains. The large body/deck relationship and simplistic bottom mechanism remain virtually unchanged.

1. **Shorten and broaden the drum; compact the triangular span.** Target has a stout silo and a shorter deck-to-body triangle. Live still has a long thin silo and a huge empty diagonal span. Reduce the vertical drum length relative to diameter and raise the lower brace attachment. Keep the deck substantial while doing this.
2. **Rebuild the crown-to-bridge transition.** Target has a narrower upper housing and sloping recessed equipment on the bridge shoulder. Live is a square tower behind a long flat box, now covered with repeated grilles. Narrow the crown and add the actual sloped transition; use unequal rooftop exhaust cylinders and equipment boxes instead of three identical square posts.
3. **Extend the drill and articulate the claws.** The drill remains a tiny nub underneath a short neck. Target has a long stepped metallic housing, visible stacked collars and a pointed terminal bit. Both claws need a second elbow and inward-pointing jaws; flat blades hanging from hinge discs do not produce that silhouette.
4. **Improve the landing plate and truss surface hierarchy.** Gray is correct, but the pad is still a flat noise field with clean thin lines. Add broad patches, broken border paint and stronger plate seams. The braces need flanged metallic edges and structural joints; the new hanging vent cabinets at the deck front add repeated clutter where target has a continuous deep beam with horizontal pipes.
5. **Localize surface wear and light.** Use darker recessed panel joints, cleaner worn edge metal and unequal grime deposits. The same fine brown mottling on every face still makes the hull read like textured stone. Amber spill under the deck and around the drill neck would add more than additional front-facing lamp dots.

## Cluster

The gray deck and additional front equipment improve material separation. The new antenna tips add interest, although they sit beside a coarse mast rather than completing the target communications assembly. Major proportions are stable from round3, so the composition score is unchanged.

1. **Expose the left connecting neck.** The left pod still meets the cube corner directly in the hero. Move it outward enough to show a short flanged corridor with a visible dark gap above and below. Right connectors need dark collars, ribs and a small attached utility run: they remain oversized plain ivory tubes.
2. **Complete the roof silhouette.** Add actual perimeter rails, several upright edge lamps and two different mast-base boxes. Target has a finer, taller aerial with many short crossmembers, a secondary mast/dish and tips extending above the truss. Current main truss still ends abruptly, and the lower mast remains short and coarse.
3. **Break up the deck at larger scales.** Target yellow edge paint is broad and chipped; current yellow outline is thin and pristine. Widen it, add interruptions and broad gray scuffed patches, retaining the clearer white inner outline.
4. **Reduce white lamp dominance and add warm spill.** The front white squares remain much brighter/larger than target fixtures. Shrink and warm them, then light adjacent pipes and metal corners subtly. Do not increase global brightness; it would flatten the pale connectors further.
5. **Use target-specific front machinery.** New vents improve density but create a repeated grille border. Add the offset left service hatch, unequal pipe bundles and a rounded underslung machinery pod. Target's dark hardware is sculpted around the main hatch rather than evenly tiled around it.

## Salvage

The heavy dark frame and dark pad correct the most important round3 mismatch. The smaller rear dish is less distracting, tower strips add depth, and lower pipes reduce the stack-of-cabinets appearance. These support the largest gain of the three. The result still has a simplified cockpit, suspended-looking tower and very straight lower silhouette.

1. **Show the tower's supports.** There is still a black horizontal gap under the cylinder and a single conspicuous right-hand truss. Add visible paired feet beneath the cylinder and a left diagonal support, tied into roof machinery. Target tower emerges from a mechanical base; live still appears perched or floating. Segment the top rim and darken its inside.
2. **Make the central pad thick and worn.** The new dark surface is good, but it still reads as a flat inset rectangle. Add a projecting beveled lip, corner mounts and exposed side thickness. Break and fade the immaculate yellow outline; use large irregular pale scuffs instead of only uniform fine mottling.
3. **Refine the cockpit silhouette and glazing.** It remains an elongated plain wedge with two huge dark windows and three equally spaced grilles. Divide wraparound windows with frames, add an upper roof shoulder/equipment assembly, and use differing recessed service panels. Keep the red stripe but vary its wear by panel.
4. **Build the left lattice connection as a volume.** The pod still looks like a plain box suspended on a few rods. Add a short deep open corridor with cross-braced sides and a dark interior; its visible gap and thickness matter more than another grille on the pod face.
5. **Replace the lower stack with unequal volumes.** The triangular plate and extra straight pipes improve the outline, but target has bulky asymmetric lower modules, curved plumbing, underslung tanks and a bent terminal keel. Offset one major block, add elbows connecting the tanks, and angle the final member inward. The current right lower box also needs stepped attachments rather than one large bare side.
6. **Differentiate exposed metal from painted skin.** Silver pipe highlights above/below the pad are a start. Extend that distinction through the lower assembly, soften broad ivory brightness, and add warm light at connections. Target recesses contain lit depth; live face-mounted lamps largely stop at their own rectangles.

## Next-round priorities

- Rig: correct drum/span proportions, crown shoulder and drill/claw silhouette.
- Cluster: visible left neck, dark connector collars, roof rails and finer complete masts.
- Salvage: tower supports, projecting chipped pad, articulated cockpit and asymmetric lower assembly.

All three benefit from localized wear and warm light falling on adjacent metal. Repeated grilles have reached diminishing returns.
