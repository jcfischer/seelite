**Verdict: real improvement, below the 8/10 fidelity gate. Stall approaching remains active; the strict equal-or-worse-after-rebuild stop condition is not met.** The score improves by 0.45 this round and 0.80 over round 6's 4.85, below one point over two rounds. Major recurring gaps remain. This reconstruction helped, but did not produce a large overall fidelity gain.

## What landed

The low, elongated glazed bridge is clearly closer to the target than the old stepped pyramid. Large inclined shoulder exchangers replace conspicuously empty armor and create the target's alternating dark/cream shoulder rhythm. The mouth now has a substantial dark collar, corner accents and recessed opening rather than a thin outline on a pale box. These are visible assembly changes, not merely surface noise.

The detail shows useful asymmetry: cylindrical service hardware on the left, access-panel/door mass in the middle, control cabinets on the right. This is more functionally differentiated than the previously described wall of identical grilles. Warm upper fixtures illuminate nearby beams, and pipe highlights, railings and supporting trusses read clearly in this close view.

## Composition and assemblies

- Hero subject remains smaller and more right-weighted than the target, with much more empty upper/left space and a near-border bow. Match subject bounds and perspective more closely through the camera; preserve the approved physical ring dimensions.
- The ring still has broad smooth hoops with sparse outer grille patches and a largely black inner wall. The target's habitat shell has large recessed radiator fields, articulated spoke roots, segmented inner walls and long inhabited light bands. This remains a major missing assembly, unchanged in visible character.
- Side panels now have better shoulder shape, but the body is still one nearly identical tall bay repeated along its length. Target bays combine nested inclined armor, substantial dark cavities, varying service equipment and more complex lower curvature. Build a few distinct bay variants and distribute them deliberately; adding another identical cabinet to every bay will not solve this.
- Collar depth helps, but its broad roof and tall cream side face remain blank. Target collar carries layered dark caps, inset equipment, chamfered framing, fitted lighting and interrupted armor zones. Break up those large masses with real recesses and fitted subassemblies.
- Bridge now has the right broad profile, but its glazing reads as an almost uninterrupted dark ribbon and its lower skirt remains a plain wedge. Add window divisions, framed recesses and fitted equipment at the base.
- Deck remains a broad flat parking-grid surface with isolated cubes. Target has deck edge machinery, narrow marking hierarchies, inset zones and service structures. Removed fairings should stay removed.

## Lighting

- Hero bay remains a mostly dark opening, whereas the target reveals warm rear/side wall architecture and crane structure from the exterior. The close detail's warm illumination is not translating into the primary image. Establish hero-visible warm wall/task light and enough restrained fill to resolve the bay depth.
- Ring interior and lower body still collapse toward black. Target retains warm inhabited strips and readable mechanical layers in those regions. Restore local value separation rather than globally raising exposure.
- Cool gray deck dominates the render; target balances warmer cream armor, subdued charcoal deck, amber machinery and richer metallic edge highlights. Tune material response and local light together.
- Star field remains much sparser than the target. Secondary priority after hull/ring/bay lighting.

## Materials and details

- Exchangers introduce useful dark grille/amber frame contrast. Nevertheless, cream armor is still dominated by a regular small tile grid and uniformly distributed black specks. Target wear accumulates at seams, fittings and edges, with multiple plate scales and dark inset layers. Make wear placement and panelization respond to assembly structure.
- Pipes have a convincing metallic response in the detail, but repeated boxes still have broad unarticulated black faces and identical three-slat fronts. Add mounts, fasteners, varied door/control arrangements and connected cable/service runs.
- The rear central assembly is six large flat slabs with no strong door frame, center mechanism, locks or operational markings. Its function remains ambiguous. Resolve it into a readable access assembly without filling the central route.
- Ceiling crane is mostly a large beam with a box and vertical rod. Give the trolley visible wheels/rail seating, bracing and a recognizable lifting mechanism. The target's crane is a clear industrial hierarchy.
- Several projecting side pipe ends face the camera as conspicuous open dark circles, while floor plates stand proud along the central lane. These attract attention without a clear functional story; fit caps/connectors/supports and clarify whether the floor pieces are intended hatches or obstructions.
- Detail camera still crops the catwalk ends and much of the crane span. Retain this close rear-wall evidence, but add a wider front-oblique shot showing mouth frame, a full catwalk, crane, floor and rear wall. No exact target interior close-up exists, so this detail confirms construction/readability rather than pixel-level interior matching.

## Next decision

Do not spend another round uniformly adding small trims. The largest remaining causes are the undeveloped inhabited ring assembly, insufficiently differentiated side bays, and lighting that hides the bay in the hero. A focused rebuild of the ring's visible inner/outer habitat shell plus hero-level lighting design would address much more of the remaining image than additional bridge or rear-wall polishing. Preserve physical floor diameter, rotation and clearance constraints. This review does not constitute principal approval and does not justify reporting target fidelity as achieved.
