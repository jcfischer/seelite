## Verdict

All three clearly belong to the Coriolis visual family now. The mottled ivory panels, near-black metalwork, layered pipe runs, framed service vents, thick aperture coaming, restrained amber fixtures and painted hazard marks transfer visibly. The previous clean, cooler and somewhat toy-like finish is substantially reduced. The three layouts remain recognisable against their previous hero frames. The Slipway aperture visibly faces upward. Connections visibly have enclosed structure; this is an exterior visual observation, not a traversal proof. Carrier dimensions and robotic motion cannot be certified from these stills.

No additional visual iteration is required to meet the user's threshold. These scores are not principal visual approval.

## Remaining gaps to perfection

### Common

- Coriolis has greater hierarchy between broad dark structural frames and inset light panels. The yard wall blocks remain more uniformly pale; selected darker structural bands around panel fields would make the transfer stronger without changing layouts.
- The hubs successfully acquire deep aperture coaming and service rails, but four identical large square corner housings and regular pipe clamps are conspicuously repetitive. Coriolis mixes fixture sizes, rectangular equipment boxes and layered rail subdivisions more richly.
- The aperture has long continuous amber strips; the reference relies more on individual inset lamps and warm light falling onto surrounding surfaces. Breaking some strips into seated fixtures and reducing their brightness would better match the reference.
- Pale panels show convincing mottling, yet nearly every exposed material has similarly distributed wear. Distinct edge wear, protected recesses, greasy pipe collars and less uniform surface roughness would improve material differentiation.
- Large rear hub discs and connector boxes are much simpler than the detailed fronts. This is apparent in the connection views, especially O2. Add a few meaningful panel divisions or service ribs if future work includes these angles.
- The Carrier remains visibly smoother and cooler than the new station. This is not a reason to change its approved scale or geometry; it merely prevents absolute whole-scene material consistency.

### O1

- Yellow gantry end blocks dominate the warm palette more than the reference's small hazard accents. They are appropriate tooling identifiers, but their uninterrupted painted faces could use inset access panels or dark reinforcement seams.
- Straight yard edges have strong panel texture but less layered framing than the hub. Thin rails improve them; a few thicker frame breaks would better distribute the Coriolis shape language across the whole station.
- Cool highlights on the Carrier and some pipe caps remain visible against otherwise warmer, rougher surfaces. This is modest and does not undermine the overall transfer.

### O2

- Large pale vertical bay buttresses and roof masses read as simple block forms. The Coriolis reference's dark framing, chamfered transitions and inset vent fields are most concentrated in the hub, not these masses.
- Bay machinery is darker and harder to distinguish than the Coriolis aperture interior. Small local amber task lights could reveal the inner robotic joints while preserving the restrained palette.
- The rear connector close-up loses much of its construction in near-black shadow. A little reflected fill would improve readability without recreating the old bright white illumination.

### O3

- The long light inner ring remains relatively continuous, while Coriolis divides large panel areas with assertive dark structural ribs. Occasional deeper dark breaks would strengthen the transfer.
- Repeated yellow manipulators draw more attention than the restrained Coriolis hardware. Their repetition is functional, but varied local shadow and subtler yellow roughness would make the assembly less uniform.
- The front fork blocks retain simple rectangular masses. Inset service plates or more evident dark corner framing could enrich their shape language without altering the approved fork layout.
- The upward aperture is convincingly deep and framed, but its continuous luminous rim is stronger than the restrained individual fixtures in the reference.

These are optional refinements above the requested threshold, not blockers.
