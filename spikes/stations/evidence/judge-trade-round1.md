## Ring

The large ring, four spokes and central docking block establish the correct family, and the frame is reasonably close. However, the target has a substantially heavier, rounder ring section and a larger central station. The implementation reads as a thin flat-sided polygonal hoop around a comparatively small, square box. Target spokes are shorter and integrate through substantial mechanical collars into both hub and ring. The target is also viewed slightly more obliquely.

Highest-impact changes:

1. Increase the hub's projected width from roughly 29% to roughly 39% of ring width; increase ring-section thickness and bevel/round its section so it reads as a substantial torus assembled from armor segments. Preserve the outer envelope while reducing the empty interior area.
2. Replace the mostly flat hub face with layered corner structure, a wider docking aperture, a projecting multi-part docking surround, and roof perimeter machinery. Make the spoke connections visibly layered rather than thin black cuffs.
3. Give the ring circumferential recessed service/window channels, raised dark hoop ribs and occasional gold stripes. Current isolated vents and little white lamps distributed on an otherwise uninterrupted pale shell do not recreate the target's construction.
4. Warm the small lights toward amber; increase their local illumination around docking and joints. Maintain dirty off-white armor but use dark metallic material on structural edges and channels. The current palette has the right foundation, but surfaces have much less depth and material separation.

## Drum

The cylinder and its bands identify the target, but the camera is too close: the front is cut off at the left edge and the hull fills nearly the whole image. The target leaves substantial space around a complete station. Its front rim is deeply beveled, the docking aperture is larger and brighter, and the barrel has nested structural bands, rails and exposed machinery. Current broad plain panels and simple black bands make it look like a minimally dressed cylinder.

Highest-impact changes:

1. Pull the camera back until the full station fits with margins comparable to the target. Target hull occupies roughly the middle 60% of image height; the current hull is approximately 80%. Preserve a three-quarter view but match the target's less extreme foreground enlargement.
2. Rebuild the front as an inset cap inside a substantial beveled perimeter. Enlarge the port relative to the cap and add a layered docking frame. Remove the giant cross-like vent assemblies: the target has narrow service runs dividing armor sectors, not large grilles dominating the front.
3. Build the long dark barrel channels as actual recessed machinery troughs with pipes, rails, small amber windows and bracketed connections. Raise and subdivide the circumference ribs instead of relying on flat black strips.
4. Reduce the repeated, oversized grille motif. Add fine panels, fastening strips and occasional yellow wear markings at differing scales. Current grime provides surface variation, but cannot supply the missing construction depth.

## Assembly

The tall truss mast, side tube, left tank and radiator panels are present. The target's identifying core, however, is a chamfered octagonal industrial hub. Current core is effectively the Ring's box with attachments. The target's side tube is a heterogeneous chain of adapters, narrow connector sections and a distinct terminal faceted pod; the implementation is two near-uniform tubes ending in a flat dark opening. Target left structure has separately articulated upper and lower tanks and multiple truss ties; the implementation is a single continuous cylinder behind the hub.

Highest-impact changes:

1. Redesign the main hub with strongly clipped corners, inset front armor quadrants and a thick multi-layer docking surround. This is a silhouette/geometry requirement, not a texture refinement.
2. Rebuild the right extension as separate modules with flange collars and exposed dark connector sections, ending in the target's enlarged faceted cap/pod. Add the upper and lower truss-supported small horizontal service modules visible beside the hub.
3. Split the left tank into upper/lower assemblies with mechanical collars and a transverse connector. Add the missing tie structure so attachments read as an engineered assembly rather than cylinders touching the box.
4. Change radiators into multiple narrow separated fins with gold end framing; move the top radiator to the left side of its mast to match the target. Add mast service boxes and small amber lights. Scale/framing should make the station wider and core slightly larger in the final image.

## Shared priority

The most valuable next round is substantial silhouette and construction work: heavier Ring, beveled Drum endcap, octagonal Assembly core and segmented attachments. Then add geometry at the intermediate scale (ribs, channels, collars, port surrounds). Existing weathered maps can remain the base. More uniform grime or more isolated vents will not close the main gap. Target lighting has amber highlights and controlled dark metal contrast; live captures are flatter, paler, and have sparse near-white lights. A denser subtle starfield would also improve fidelity, but is secondary to the station geometry.
