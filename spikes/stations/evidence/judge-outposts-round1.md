## Rig — prioritized changes

1. **Restructure the silo and its crown.** The target has a broad lower drum with a shoulder, a narrower tall equipment housing rising above the pad, and a dense mechanical neck below. Live uses a near-constant cylinder with a flat concentric lid approximately at pad level. Build those three distinct volumes before adding surface noise. Shorten the uninterrupted drum and reserve more vertical extent for the lower machinery and articulated claws.
2. **Deepen the landing platform and attachment.** Target pad has a substantial layered perimeter, inset girders/pipes, corner armor blocks, and an enclosed sloping service bridge into the tower. Live is a shallow slab connected by a flat blank strip. Target triangular support has heavy plated beams and visible smaller braces; live looks like thin bridge lattice, much too open and visually light. Add a thick plated lower diagonal and broad flanged upper diagonals, with a few open triangles.
3. **Replace blank cylinder area with equipment at distinct depths.** Add vertical recessed service bays, irregular armor plates, a broad mid-height belt, raised rectangular side tanks in two tiers, pipe runs, and a ribbed lower side radiator. Target side equipment is box-like; the three live side bottles do not reproduce it.
4. **Rebuild the lower mechanism.** Target has a long telescopic drill and bulky jointed grippers with multiple hinge discs, dark inset machinery and yellow hazard ends. Live tiny angular claws and short cylinder lose that entire industrial silhouette.
5. **Presentation and finish.** Pull the hero camera back until the full pad fits. Remove the single intense yellow pad-center reflection/glow; use restrained warm perimeter lights. Break up uniform dirty ivory with cleaner edge wear, dark equipment recesses, larger irregular panel divisions and subtle roughness variation. Target pad has a pale central landing outline and localized worn yellow perimeter markings, not a regular white tile grid.

## Cluster — prioritized changes

1. **Add the missing depth hierarchy to the cube.** Target front has a central recessed rectangular hatch, deep offset equipment frame on the left, pipes along the right, a heavy external corner skeleton and a projecting underslung utility pod. Live cube has flat tiled walls with repeated grilles and glowing rectangles. The outer silhouette is reasonably close, but the body reads as a plain box. Model the front hatch and frame first; then external ribs, offsets and bottom pod.
2. **Correct module reach and shaping.** Target left upright pod stands clearly apart on a visible collar/connector; live left pod is nearly flush against the cube in hero view. Increase that separation. The right pods should have rounded/beveled shoulders and substantial connector flange assemblies, rather than flat stepped lids and mostly plain tubes. Reduce lower-right pod height slightly relative to the body and expose its connector.
3. **Develop the deck and mast.** Target has thin perimeter railings, lit upright posts, layered edge machinery and two equipment cabinets at the mast base. Replace the bright regular deck grid with dark broad panel variation, pale central outline and worn perimeter paint. Add fine mast subdivisions, aerials and small warm markers; live mast is an oversized sparse truss. Preserve lower mast visibility through framing.
4. **Reduce emissive dominance and improve material scale.** Large bright wall rectangles read as generic lights, unlike the target's small inset amber fixtures. Lower their area/brightness and mount them in housings. Use broad panel variation plus controlled edge wear, with darker frames and cleaner metallic pipe highlights. Rear screenshot confirms most apparent detail is one repeated texture over a large flat wall; detail cannot be supplied by dirt alone.

## Salvage — prioritized changes

1. **Rebuild the body as an accumulation of parts.** Target center is a dense open industrial frame surrounding a raised thick landing plate, backed by offset equipment. Live is one large flat rectangular slab with boxes attached directly. Add multiple front depth levels: outer dark structural frame, inset wall/recess, proud pad plate with thick edge, brackets and surrounding equipment. Separate side modules with short visible lattice/corridors instead of fused flush boxes.
2. **Correct the ship fragment.** Target upper-right nose is an old small spacecraft section: multiple sloping facets, raised roof, recessed wraparound cockpit glazing and narrow faded red stripe on mostly ivory hull. Live is a huge flat orange wedge with two rectangular windows visibly standing proud. Make the nose narrower in height, facet its shoulders, recess glazing into an angled cockpit band, and limit red to a weathered stripe.
3. **Replace lower box with tapered mechanical silhouette.** Target underside descends through several narrowing modules into a hook-like keel, with tanks, curved pipes and machinery layered at the sides. Live ends in one rectangular orange block. Add two or three stepped volumes, narrow the bottom, and place asymmetric equipment around the taper.
4. **Rebuild tower and upper deck.** Target upper cylinder is open at top, with interior darkness and rim thickness; live has a closed stepped lid. Add the open rim, vertical vent/inset, support legs on both sides, rear circular dish and surface pipework. Reduce the plain height of the cylinder and break its connection to the roof with a visible machinery base.
5. **Materials and lighting.** Reduce saturated orange area substantially; target paint is mostly dirty ivory with faded rust-red bands and dark framing. Add localized chipped paint on the landing plate (target is heavily scuffed), panel-specific wear and brighter metallic pipe edges. Add small warm fixtures around pad and within recesses; current sparse pinlights do not articulate the object. Uniform dirt on large planar areas gives little of the target's physical detail.

## Shared next-round order

Fix primary and secondary volumes first, then model dark recesses and protruding equipment, then tune material variation and lights. More uniform grilles, dirt or brightness alone will not close the gap. Preserve uncropped hero framing with comparable object scale and view angle for the next comparison.
