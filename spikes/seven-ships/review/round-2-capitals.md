## Shared repeated blockers

- **Camera fit remains wrong.** Targets run approximately x=30–1490 and y=140–880. These run x=335–1360 and y=300–930. Ship is still too small, too far right and too low. Increase projected size about 1.4× and center the projected bounding box; camera aiming at model origin is evidently not producing target framing. Aesthetic judgment at unequal size conceals needed structure.
- **Unbroken tube remains the dominant form.** Target has deep layered shoulders, armor frames integrated through side and roof, inset mechanical belts and large designed bow transitions. Current result is still a single perfectly regular shell with surface attachments.
- **Lighting has almost no improved local depth.** Target contains many dark cavities against warm bevel highlights. Current roof is a flat, evenly lit gray grid, new blocks have broad featureless faces, and warm lights provide too little perceptual depth.
- **Material hierarchy is still absent.** Large grid cells follow one regular subdivision everywhere; target alternates differently sized light armored panels, dark exposed channels, local repair plates and coarse purposeful paint patches. Adding noise or shrinking a uniform grid will not solve this.
- **Repeated fixtures are too identical.** Targets use a coherent construction rhythm but every bay is functionally populated; current ribs, vents, lamps and boxes repeat with a stamped rhythm. Prioritize a few large real assemblies over another row of identical tiny details.

## Carrier — architectural correction

Current enlarged cockpit and deck-edge lamps do not fix the three dominant discrepancies:

1. **Deck is still a pale flat lid.** Make the deck itself a dark charcoal recessed working surface, integrate its sides into thick beveled shoulder strips, and introduce three scales of lane markings: long paired yellow lines, short crosswise service marks and framed landing zones. Target perimeter carries continuous rails and nonuniform service clusters; isolated identical lamp boxes currently emphasize the floating rectangular slab.
2. **Hull bays need stepped armor construction.** Build broad dark structural hoops as multi-face armor pieces around the cross-section; put subtly bowed/chamfered ivory bays between them with distinct upper vent housings. Current skinny black strips, even with new lower vent boxes, remain straps on a tube. Create a recessed continuous mid-height channel holding two or three pipe runs and larger service cabinets at selected bays.
3. **Hangar requires a new interior assembly.** Current bay has a floor X, flat ledges and few slabs. Target reads as a huge occupied workshop through a thick armored aperture: deep inset jambs, overhead yellow gantry with triangular braces, multi-level side catwalk, equipment walls, ceiling lights, floor lanes and small craft. Rebuild as nested architectural volumes with actual interior depth; ensure warm lights reveal the far wall and dark occlusion distinguishes each level.

Bridge is now larger but dominated by an oversized blue glass wedge. Target has a terraced bridge island with a thin dark wraparound window band and substantial opaque roof, ancillary blocks, dome and antenna mast. Reshape its proportions; avoid further scaling the current cockpit.

Carrier materials remain wrong: darken deck, break ivory plates into larger purpose-shaped regions, remove broad noisy mottling, keep wear concentrated at corners/seams, put ochre on substantial corner armor and machinery fittings. Rear pods still read like grille boxes rather than armored barrels. Bow frame still reads as a thin yellow outline.

## Dreadnought — architectural correction

Added roof arches and turrets address the right feature class, hence composition improves. But:

1. **Arches are disconnected-looking brackets above a flat roof.** They must be continuations of broad full-height structural frames, with thick chamfered tops and shoulders defining recessed weapon bays. Currently skinny side ribs end below broad new upper brackets, creating an obvious hierarchy discontinuity. Rebuild each structural hoop as one coherent cross-sectional assembly and build roof panels between hoops, lower than hoop tops.
2. **Turrets are undetailed cuboids on smooth oval plinths.** Target uses angular armored gun houses with collar recesses and clearly visible twin long barrels. Current small dark stubs are visually lost. Give each turret a low faceted housing, dark barrel sockets, long paired metallic barrels and a stepped bearing base; keep it partially down inside a well rather than resting on a smooth pad atop a roof.
3. **Bridge has become nearly invisible between roof blocks.** Target bridge is the dominant raised center/aft mass, with high tiered opaque armor and slim windows. Build a substantial central bridge tier above the battery line and connect it to a longitudinal armored spine.
4. **Bow has acquired a gear-like ring of blocks.** That is not the target's integrated weapon cheek structure. Target armor tapers into a deep axial weapon recess, with lengthwise flanking supports and an upper gun cutout; current attached cubes around a flat end cap accentuate a toy gear. Replace front cap assembly with nested tapered armor cheeks, two long flank supports, deep concentric barrel recess and bronze internal rings. Cut a visible upper recess into the nose shoulder for the twin-gun mount.

Dreadnought color hierarchy: target dark graphite roof with irregular ivory armor bands and large muted rust-red patches. Current pale roof grid and small rust tabs survive almost unchanged. Recompose large plate materials first, then add finer seams. Side surfaces need deep dark machinery channels and offset layered armor rather than uniformly tiled uninterrupted planes.

## Required next-pass strategy

Freeze extra small-detail work. Correct camera. Construct the carrier as an armored hangar architecture with integrated flight deck; construct the dreadnought as a sequence of structural hoops, recessed battery bays, bridge spine and layered weapon bow. Use those assemblies to replace large surfaces of the current shell. Reassign large surface color blocks to target before restoring micro-wear. Then capture both at target framing and judge again.
