## Rig

**Improvement:** full platform now fits; the crown has stepped volumes; side cabinets and vents interrupt the blank drum; pad edges have more depth; the distracting pad-center highlight and white tile grid are gone.

1. **Framing overcorrected.** Target fills roughly nine tenths of the image width; round2 spans roughly three fifths. Bring the camera closer until the complete pad and grippers fill the image with a modest margin. The target tower is broad and its supporting arm compact; the live drum remains a tall narrow silo with a disproportionately long open triangular span.
2. **Crown is still the wrong structure.** Several short concentric tiers resemble a lid. Target has a substantial narrow upright housing above the broad drum, projecting service bridge with sloping equipment faces, and roof machinery. Raise that upper housing; make the bridge enclosed and deep, with an angled transition into it.
3. **Understructure needs mass.** The thin repetitive trusses still dominate the pad-to-drum gap. Target has broad armored diagonals, smaller inner braces, flanges and a heavy bottom chord. Cover some lattice with substantial beams while retaining intentional openings.
4. **Lower mechanism is still abbreviated.** The target has a long busy neck, stepped drill and large jointed grippers. Live still ends in a short center stub and simple small angular claws. Allocate significantly more silhouette height to these assemblies; add hinge discs and dark mechanical recesses, then localized hazard paint.
5. **Finish and deck:** newly repeated black vents help contrast but do not reproduce irregular layered equipment or box-shaped two-tier side tanks. Target deck is readable gray with a broad pale landing outline; live is near-black with a thin yellow rectangle and cross. Restore broad tonal panel variation and the pale inner landing boundary. Keep warm fixtures restrained but let their local light reveal structure.

## Cluster

**Improvement:** framed front hatch, vertical utilities and underslung pod now articulate the cube. Pod-mounted equipment adds depth. Removing the uniform deck grid and center glare was useful.

1. **Module separation remains the main silhouette error.** Left pod is mostly behind the body and almost flush to its corner. Right pods remain crowded together. Target shows a clearly separated left pod and long, exposed, flanged right connectors with ample black space between modules. Extend the connectors and adjust placement so those negative spaces survive the hero angle.
2. **The front hatch is oversized and too dark.** It fills much of the face like a giant door. Target hatch is a modest central rectangular assembly with generous ivory panels around it, offset left equipment and right pipes. Reduce hatch area, expose surrounding wall and break its uniform black plane into inset panels and edge hardware.
3. **Framing and mast:** round2 subject occupies only about half the width versus most of the target. Move closer after widening the module layout. The target lower mast is clearly visible below the cube; live has a short dark fragment behind the bottom pod. Extend/show that mast. Upper mast still lacks the target finer crossmembers, secondary aerial and paired base equipment.
4. **Deck remains unfinished relative to target.** The new black empty plane loses the target scuffed gray landing surface, pale inner outline and broad faded perimeter markings. Add railings and upright light posts rather than only corner blocks. Target shows a layered deck lip; continue that treatment around the entire visible perimeter.
5. **Lighting remains too binary.** Large white wall rectangles still dominate dark front equipment. Reduce their area and warm them; give metal corners and pipes enough fill/specular response to show shape. The target balances visible ivory wall, gray machinery and localized amber light; live groups those into bright tiles and near-black accessories.

## Salvage

**Improvement:** front perimeter now has pipes, rails and layered framework; more small warm lights reveal its edges; added tower and side equipment improves local richness. This earns a modest gain, not a solved silhouette.

1. **Ship fragment remains a large orange wedge.** Added separate rectangular window blocks make the cockpit less credible: they stand proud in an irregular cluster. Rebuild the nose with sloping shoulders, raised roof and recessed angled wraparound glazing. Make most of the hull ivory and restrict faded red to a horizontal stripe. This is a primary target-identity requirement.
2. **Lower silhouette remains a rectangular orange box.** Target descends through several asymmetric stepped volumes into a narrow hooked keel, with side tanks and bent pipes. Replace the single lower box with a tapering sequence and a narrow offset terminal member. Surface grilles cannot fix this outline.
3. **Tower is still closed with a concentric lid.** Target is visibly open-topped, shorter relative to the body and supported by substantial legs on both sides; a dish rises behind it. Make a genuinely open dark interior with a thick rim, expose the machinery base and add the paired supports and dish.
4. **Assemblage still reads as flush-mounted blocks.** Target left modules stand off on visible lattice corridors; current boxes touch the central frame. Create gaps and connective structures, with offset heights/depths. Use different local equipment arrangements rather than the same three horizontal cabinets on every box.
5. **Front pad and material scale:** target pad is a thick projecting worn plate with chipped yellow marking and surrounding recessed machinery; live is still a flat nearly black rectangle with a clean thin yellow line. Add bevel/side depth, broad gray wear patches and uneven paint loss. Current repeated fine dirt gives every material the same grain. Separate hull paint, dark structural metal, bright pipe edges and worn plate roughness.

## Highest-impact next pass

First rebuild the three silhouette-defining misses: Rig lower mechanism and crown/bridge, Cluster spaced pods and visible lower mast, Salvage cockpit and tapered keel. Then match hero size and deck/pad tones. Adding further repeated cabinets without those structural changes is unlikely to move any subject to 7.
