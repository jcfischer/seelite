## Drum

The more horizontal axis and faceted front shoulder improve the principal silhouette. The warm enclosed bay is a substantial improvement over the empty frames, although it now reads as a brightly illuminated shallow box. The barrel still lacks the target layered machinery and surface hierarchy.

Priority gaps:

1. Build substantial continuous dark cross members on the endcap, reaching from rim to docking surround. Embed pipes of differing diameters, clamps and amber fittings. Current isolated vent stacks on pale sheets lack the target structural cross; this is a large, visible face-level discrepancy.
2. Make the broad front bevel dark armored facets, with seams and smaller attached plates. The new faceting is right, but the upper bevel remains a broad pale strip and the dark lower shoulder remains largely blank.
3. Give the bay visible depth: panel the interior walls, add ceiling ribs and a floor threshold, and spread the amber lighting across the floor and sidewall instead of the intense upper-left wall patch. Reduce the oversized white corner fixtures.
4. Break up the broad empty black band above the middle pipe lane with integrated recess sidewalls, narrow window boxes and equipment. The target has dense layered axial troughs, not a wide plain stripe with most machinery along its lower edge.
5. Strengthen localized specular metal edges and shadow separation. The repeated dirt texture supplies wear but most surfaces still share similar rough diffuse behavior.

## Assembly

The two service pods no longer visibly float. The middle right module is longer and slimmer, and the terminal has regained an octagonal outline. These fix real round 4 regressions. Warm hangar light and mast boxes also help. However, the terminal has become a large nearly bare pale drum; the lower service pod overlaps the hanging tank instead of reading as a distinct pod within the truss span.

Priority gaps:

1. Finish the terminal pod. Keep the octagonal geometry, but add a dark segmented perimeter frame, cross straps, collars, amber fixtures and end-face equipment. The target terminal is a densely framed capsule; the current blank pale block is conspicuous at hero scale.
2. Move the lower horizontal service pod inward along the lower truss so it is clearly between the hub and hanging tank, without silhouette overlap. Trim the truss projecting beyond the hanging tank. Likewise expose a little more of the upper service pod and supporting span instead of burying most of it against the hub roof.
3. Replace radiator central gold squares with thin gold perimeter edges and four or five narrower dark fin panels. All three panels still visibly diverge from the target.
4. Give mast equipment boxes amber lamps and contrasting edge hardware. The new dark cubes interrupt the truss but do not reproduce the target bright serviced junctions.
5. Reduce the large black triangular void in the hangar with coherent wall and ceiling surfaces. The new warm floor helps, but the opening still reads as a wedge rather than the target deep enclosed room.
6. Carry the hanging tank a little farther right and refine the collars between axial modules. Current large pale barrel sections and coarse neck rings still compress the target articulated sequence.
