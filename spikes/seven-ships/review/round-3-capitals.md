## Verdict

Carrier improves modestly: dark deck and a stepped opaque bridge establish better functional separation. Dreadnought regresses slightly in target fidelity: bridge improves, but the new separated blunt bow block moves away from the target's continuous tapered, integrated nose. Neither reaches the 8/10 gate. Dreadnought meets the stalled condition after the architectural attempt and a lower score. Carrier's score rose, so it does not meet that exact same-or-worse test, but its main architectural deficiencies remain and progress is only 0.4 points.

The attempted architectural changes have not solved the primary mismatch. Both still have broad uninterrupted tiled bodies decorated with thin strips and identical appliances. Added segmentation in the dreadnought breaks silhouette continuity without producing the target's coherent structural armor hierarchy. This is not a materials-only or missing-small-details problem.

## Persistent shared blocker: framing

Current ships remain at roughly x=335–1360, y=305–950; targets fill approximately x=30–1490, y=140–880. This is the third verdict identifying the same easy-to-see camera gap. The subjects need roughly 1.4× projected width and a center farther up and left. Frame actual projected visible bounds; do not keep accepting origin-centered camera placement. The target nose-to-tail foreshortening and visible roof/side ratios should then be matched.

## Carrier

### What improved

- Deck is now clearly darker than the ivory shell, closer to target palette.
- Bridge has opaque stepped mass and a narrow window band rather than oversized fighter-like glass.
- New mast reads as naval equipment.

### What remains structurally wrong

1. Deck is still a thin oversized flat plate laid on the hull. Target has a dark working surface bounded by integrated thick armored shoulders, continuous rails and irregular service islands. Current pale slab border and rows of identical lamp boxes emphasize the lid construction.
2. Main side ribs remain slender strips. Target has broad dark load-bearing hoops around heavy beveled ivory bays, with shoulders changing the silhouette. Increase frame width substantially and make roof/side/lower armor sections one coherent member.
3. Side wall still reads as a perfectly flat repetitive facade. Target has a recessed machinery belt, branching pipes with couplers, larger isolated ochre cabinets and varying panel widths. A row of identical lower vent rectangles does not establish mechanical depth.
4. Hangar still has thin trim, an empty dark rectangular cavity, floor cross marks and flat ledges. Target has thick nested jambs, multi-tier catwalks, a major yellow overhead truss crane, warm lit equipment walls and small ships. This was the central carrier architectural request in round 2; it remains almost unchanged.
5. Bridge is a symmetric stacked pyramid. Target is an elongated terraced command island with a lower forward equipment section, darker roof and rear domed/antenna details. Current almost all-ivory tiers lack large value separation.
6. Rear engine pods still read predominantly as paired vent boxes. Target has large faceted armored barrels with dark end caps and warm inset vertical windows.

### Lighting/material gaps

Deck has improved value but lacks target's painted lane hierarchy and surface detail. White plating still uses uniform square grid and generalized grit. Target has purposeful dark shoulder plates, restrained ochre corner panels and varied plate sizes. Deep contact shadows, warm hangar lighting and exposed metal reflections are still missing.

## Dreadnought

### What improved

Bridge now rises above the battery line and has a tiered opaque naval shape. Some large side segmentation creates greater depth than the previous unbroken tube.

### What regressed

The bow has become a detached-looking octagonal plug connected by a narrow neck, capped by a gear-like ring of cubes. Target has a long continuous armored taper with layered cheeks surrounding a deep bronze weapon throat. The new abrupt exposed notch is not in the target and weakens the massive integrated silhouette. This outweighs the bridge gain in composition.

### Remaining structural causes

1. Broad upper brackets still terminate above skinny side strips. Target's heavy continuous transverse armor arches should define the entire cross-section and carry into the sides. Current hierarchy disconnect is obvious.
2. Weapon wells are not actually wells: smooth oval pads and plain rectangular turrets sit atop a pale roof plane. Target weapon housings are faceted, partially sunk between raised armor shoulders, with long visible paired barrels. Build the bays as bounded recessed spaces and replace turret cuboids with their own angular assemblies.
3. New body segment boundaries introduce regular exposed dark gaps; target has broad continuous armor interrupted by purposeful recesses, not separate block cars. Restore overall tapered continuity and obtain depth through layered plates and inset machinery.
4. Bow needs complete reconsideration. Remove the plug/neck articulation and radial square blocks. Use a tapered cheek assembly, longitudinal supports, nested axial chamber rings, recessed warm bronze throat and upper gun recess.
5. Bridge is too uniform in footprint and finish. Target spreads into a longitudinal central spine with ancillary housings and darker upper masses.
6. Side shell remains a grid wall with identical vents and fine pipes. Target has broad irregular ivory patches over charcoal armor, larger red accent panels and deep black machinery channels.

### Lighting/material gaps

Pale roof grid, featureless dark turret blocks and uniform square side cells persist. Bow interior is still a dark unlit ring instead of warm metal depth. Side values are increasingly crushed while top blocks are uniformly light; target separates armor planes and recesses locally. Darkening the overall hull does not substitute for that separation.

## Recommendation

Do not claim visual convergence. Dreadnought should pause for principal input under the stalled exit criterion. Show target and latest screenshot together and ask whether the intended style or construction approach should change. Carrier may technically continue because its score improved, but further generic fixtures would repeat the same failure; its unresolved hangar and integrated armor framing require substantive reconstruction before another useful review.
