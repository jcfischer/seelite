**Derrick reaches the requested 7.0 stop threshold. Wheel and Buoy do not.** Threshold attainment is a judgment of approximate visual fidelity, not equivalence to the target or principal visual approval. Scores are not required to rise: Wheel gains mechanical detail but loses some useful mouth lighting.

## Wheel

The interrupted ring lamps are closer to the target, and the larger dark mouth frame has greater mechanical weight. Framing remains good. However, the dark frame consumes much of the hub face and the mouth has lost the target warm interior emphasis. The hub still reads as a small rectangular tunnel housing instead of a broad layered barrel. The target ring has heavier discrete inhabited segments, especially along its inner axial depth; current ring remains a relatively thin, smooth belt. Added details do not resolve this mass distribution.

Priority gaps:

1. Widen the hub outer barrel/collar without enlarging the aperture again. Make its silhouette clearly stepped, with the rear collar protruding above and below the front shell. Preserve pale structural cheeks around the dark mouth frame.
2. Restore localized warm illumination inside the mouth and immediately around its bevels; the reference mouth is a focal point, whereas this frame is dark and reveals a plain ramp.
3. Add large transverse ring joints with measurable projection and alternate substantial cover plates along the inner rear truss. That truss is still almost empty triangle repetition.
4. Reveal vertical spoke equipment with restrained midtone metal faces and small warm fixtures. Avoid the existing nearly black machinery column beside a uniformly pale strip.
5. Add seam-localized wear and broader plate-value variation. Current uniform speckling still reads flatter than the reference assembled plates and recesses.

## Derrick

The dark landing surface corrects a large material/value mismatch. Raised tank valves and connecting pipes remove the black-disk placeholder appearance. Deeper fascia modules, base equipment and longer core lights provide meaningful mechanical depth. These combined corrections justify reaching 7.0. It remains visibly simpler than the reference, especially in the deck thickness, tank pipework, hut and landing-surface treatment.

Remaining gaps, if further work is desired:

1. Integrate fascia depth into a continuous structural belt. The new low-hanging vent boxes resemble separate teeth; the target has a thick connected deck with braces and recessed bays.
2. Break the clean white pad rectangle into worn paint and add large irregular plate changes. The surface now has the right approximate darkness but remains uniformly tiled and flat.
3. Add supported valve service platforms and brighter curved pipe elbows around tank shoulders. Current dark neck stacks are legible but still very coarse.
4. Rework the hut into a lower, wider industrial equipment cabin with side boxes and smaller fixtures. Four bright windows plus four similarly bright antenna tips give it an overly regular domestic appearance.
5. Keep the improved core lamps, but reveal midtone machinery behind the braces. The target has layered structure throughout the central shaft.

## Buoy

The upper radiator is now rooted visibly into the roof and shows the correct ribbed face; this resolves the strongest prior defect. The lower radiator is tilted and the pale landing slab is corrected to charcoal. Added lower horizontal tanks improve the body silhouette. However, the body remains a largely plain cylinder with sparse small machinery, the hatch is still dominated by four continuous bright strips, and the deck fascia repeats the same hanging vent modules rather than the target load-bearing braces. The lower radiator is also nearly edge-on, so its ribbed face does not register well.

Priority gaps:

1. Replace the hatch continuous luminous strips with shorter warm fixtures and build dark beveled corner brackets and top/bottom pipe runs around it. The target hatch is an assembled mechanism; current hatch remains a bright rectangular picture frame.
2. Add diagonal braces from deck corners to body piers and pair them with visible service pipe elbows. This is a large recognizable target feature missing from the current roof/body junction.
3. Reorient the bottom radiator slightly toward the camera so its ribbed face is visible rather than a narrow pale edge. Add a substantial perimeter frame and hinge supports to the right radiator, which still reads as a thin dark shelf.
4. Increase lower equipment depth: broad inset boxes and a service recess between the new tanks, with dark structural backing. The two white tanks alone do not create the target machinery belt.
5. Vary body plate values and local roughness while reducing uniform dirt flecking. Keep pale shell versus dark pad separation.
6. Reduce the pad and hut visual simplicity with broken paint, layered perimeter rails, and a lower wider cabin. Current four antenna lights also draw more attention than their reference counterparts.

## Shared observation

The sparse pinprick starfield and nearly absolute black background still differ from the richer reference fields. This is secondary to missing structural depth. More overall brightness, additional identical vent boxes or more speckle would not close the remaining gaps.
